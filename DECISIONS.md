# Decisions

One dated entry per decision that shapes the product, newest first: what was decided, by whom, and why. This file exists because the 2026-07-27 assumption audit showed decisions scattered across five documents and chat, which is how observations silently harden into rules. New decisions land here the day they are made; older ones migrate when a document touching them is edited anyway. The standard here is the lightweight ADR (Architecture Decision Record) practice, ours-sized.

- **2026-08-06 — Hidde (evening):** Depth on the demand cities becomes the phase: Rome, Lisbon and Porto first, made genuinely good, over more breadth ("mijn instinct zegt dat het beter is om nu een aantal steden gewoon heel veel beter te gaan maken"). Supersedes the 2026-08-02 Spain-and-Japan focus. The data behind his instinct: 46 of the 56 major register-country cities are live, the remaining broad candidates are places nobody searches for, and the only demand signals on the site point at Rome (77 impressions, 0 clicks), Lisbon (only converting page) and Porto (finished 17-tree walk, 4 photos). - **2026-08-06 — Hidde:** The assembly line replaces the do-everything pass, because tokens per tree must come down (32,870 on 2026-08-05 against 19,000 the week before, measured in RETRO.md). Five parts, detailed in CLAUDE.md Step 1: briefs are generated by `passcheck.py --brief` rather than written from memory; passes split into verification (BRIEF_RESEARCH.md, facts only, Sonnet) and writing (BRIEF_WRITING.md, 15 to 20 trees batched, strong model); deterministic work is scripts; no pass under six expected candidates; photo retrieval moves to `photo_hunt.py` and a queue, with judgement in a periodic viewing pass. Target on the meter: 15,000 tokens per published tree or less. Grew out of his three observations this day (runs leaving on wrong premises, findings not read back from the database at the moment they matter, over-strict rule readings like Zaragoza), each confirmed by the retro arithmetic.

- **2026-08-06 — Hidde:** A tree that cannot be re-confirmed alive does not hold a city back. The dead-tree rule is about an entry, not about a confidence threshold for publishing, and Zaragoza had been held back on the second reading. The page publishes and states plainly what it does not know, names what that source has already lost, and asks readers to report a tree that is gone; a known-dead tree still never ships and a reported one comes down the same day. His framing: 80/20, and users may point these out.

- **2026-08-06 — Hidde:** A run may act on search evidence by itself: read Search Console, find a query the site does not serve, and build or retarget the page, with no approval step. Two boundaries remain, a new page TYPE still needs a blueprint contract and therefore his yes, and the evidence must be a real query with impressions rather than a guess. Recorded with the lesson from the first use: the reported gap ("ancient tree map") was a page that already existed, so the check is against the built site, not the data files.

- **2026-08-05 — Hidde, later the same day:** Nothing researched is thrown away. Every tree a run comes across goes into the database whether or not it is ready to publish ("ik wil geen onderzoek meer voor niks zijn geweest"), with publishing kept as a separate decision from keeping. Also that day: the register map layer goes back on, not because the interaction is solved but so he can look at 6,266 designated trees beside our own and let that shape the design; and scattered rural registers like Navarra are worth having even when no city page falls out of them, because the map serves "a remarkable tree near me" and only a page needs a walk.

- **2026-08-05 — Hidde:** Register clusters are ranked by whether people search for the place, not by how cheap they are. The cheapest cluster is a village with many designated trees and no traveller, so the sort is tourist-first, then density, then tightness (scripts/backlog.py). Same session, four more of his rulings. Places are matched to published cities by DISTANCE, never by name, after two passes were dispatched to "open" Napoli and Firenze while Naples and Florence had been live for weeks. Three photos per city per pass is the cap, because photo hunting expands to fill any time it is given. The year chart on a tree page draws how much there is to SEE per month rather than leaf cover, since an evergreen drew a straight line and "dat zegt ook niets"; each moment carries a judgement (unseen, nice, striking, worth the trip) and the two stacked season charts became one. And the app landing page sells rather than apologises: its old copy opened with "There is nothing to download yet" and closed by explaining that nothing there was waiting on the app, which is three sentences talking the reader out of caring on the one page built to make them want it.

- **2026-08-04 — Hidde:** Coverage works from the supply side rather than a quota: pick up the trees that are cheaply available and develop those, instead of forcing ten per city everywhere. Recorded with the one correction he accepted, that the unit is the cheapest CLUSTER rather than the cheapest tree, because optimising for cheap alone produces good trees in scattered towns and a walk nowhere. Same session: expensive agent work gets its cost stated and his answer before it starts, after four unannounced dispatches in one day; and the week's token measurement showed register scouting is twenty-five times cheaper per tree than research and compounds, while research does not.
- **2026-08-03 — Hidde:** The coverage method changes to register-first, cluster-over-count, batched by country ("ik zeg ja tegen deze koers"). The measurement that settled it: across 81 published cities, Cadiz spans 600 metres and Kyoto 35 kilometres, and it is density rather than count that makes a city page read as a walk. His framing of the endgame stands behind it: featured trees that make walking routes possible, with ten too few for a Tokyo and too many for a small town, so the numbers move later. The ten-per-city default is unchanged for now; what changes is which ten and how they are found.
- **2026-08-02 — Hidde:** Ten per city becomes a working default rather than a hard cap. His reasoning, recorded in his words: the ten exists for "SEO strategie en haalbaarheid", while the actual vision is "dat er een behapbaar aantal bomen in de buurt te vinden zijn, geen exacte nummers". Ten stays the standard for new cities so coverage moves fast and pages stay completable; cities grow past it when remarkable trees turn up later or readers send them in. Supersedes the 2026-07-26 "the cap never moves" formulation, and with it the two-tier argument in BACKLOG.md: a finite collectible list is still the goal, it is simply per-city-honest rather than fixed at a number. Padding remains forbidden; a page that passes ten must stop claiming ten.
- **2026-08-01 — Hidde:** The day-trip boundary replaces the strict city line ("dagtrip telt mee"): a tree reachable in roughly 30 minutes by public transport from the city centre may ship on the city page, provided the entry is honest about where it stands (real place name in the location, true travel time in transport). Supersedes the strict-boundary convention that excluded Blarney from Cork, Rouken Glen from Glasgow, Coole Park from Galway and the Wodanseiken from Arnhem; those exclusions are re-openable leads, not settled facts. First application: Cork ships with the Witch's Yew and the Western Red Cedar at Blarney (bus 215, ~30 min) and the 1870 magnolia at Fota (13-minute train). Chosen over a Cork-only exception explicitly, for consistency.
