# Decisions

One dated entry per decision that shapes the product, newest first: what was decided, by whom, and why. This file exists because the 2026-07-27 assumption audit showed decisions scattered across five documents and chat, which is how observations silently harden into rules. New decisions land here the day they are made; older ones migrate when a document touching them is edited anyway. The standard here is the lightweight ADR (Architecture Decision Record) practice, ours-sized.

- **2026-08-14 — Hidde (session), executed same day: AllTrails' funnel researched on their live site and applied.** What their trail page actually does, verified by clicking it logged-out: (1) the nav on every page carries Explore, SAVED, Get the app and Log in, so the product permanently advertises that it remembers you; (2) the trail page's one action surface is a dark floating capsule with [Save, green, FIRST] [Get directions] [Send to phone]: capture beats routing; (3) every gated tap (Save, Send to phone, Open in app) converges on ONE signup modal whose warmest button is "Continue in app", so the login push and the app push are a single surface. Applied to us: the white bar and the desktop action row were replaced by one dark capsule on every viewport (save first in green, directions, walk), Saved trees joined the nav menu, and the first save opens the one dialog that sells keeping-it-everywhere with the app as its ask. **One deliberate divergence, to flip when accounts open:** AllTrails blocks the save until you sign up; ours saves instantly and locally and shows the dialog once, because a gate in front of a feature we cannot yet redeem (no accounts until deletion works, hard rule 1) would be a toll without a road. When the account track opens, the dialog's ask becomes the login and the flip is one line. Send to phone stays unbuilt pending a QR decision (hard rule 5).

- **2026-08-14 — Hidde (session): the tree page's mobile actions copy AllTrails' funnel, and "Collect this tree" leaves the mobile web.** His observation, scrolling AllTrails: their trail page carries a hanging bottom bar with Get Directions, Send to Phone and Open in App, "die funnel zou ik graag na willen maken". And his verdict on our current button: "de Collect this tree knop is op mobiel totaal onlogisch... die moeten we als lekkermakertje voor de app maken. Maar ik denk niet dat we dit in de mobiele website moeten doen." So the direction, to build next: (1) a sticky bottom action bar on the tree page at phone width, Directions primary plus the app step; (2) collect stops pretending to be a website feature and becomes the app's selling point, shown as a teaser rather than an action button; (3) desktop gets Send to Phone in AllTrails' sense, which for an account-less site means a QR/share step, since "send to phone" on a phone is meaningless. Lists of favourite trees are noted as a real reason people will eventually log in: that belongs to the accounts track and phase 2, parked, not built. This slots into the standing convention rule (AllTrails is a named reference product) and needs the whole-page check against PRODUCT_IA.md before the bar ships.

- **2026-08-14 — Hidde (session): the database is the third goal, subordinate by design.** His words: "uiteindelijk maken we de rijkste database van bomen worth visiting... alles wat we doen in searches opslaan voor de toekomst... uiteindelijk een synergy tussen gebruiker en AI: wij zetten het op en laten het verbeteren en valideren door de gebruiker." Ranked beneath goal 1 (a person in front of a tree) and goal 2 (money) at his own invitation, because a database without visitors is MonumentalTrees, the competitor we beat on experience. What it binds: (1) **the standing tiebreaker** — when two ways of doing a piece of work are otherwise equal, choose the one that leaves the database richer; (2) **no bare facts** — every stored fact carries provenance (source, register id, date), because that is what makes a later user correction mergeable; (3) **the schema never assumes city** — cities are the front door, but leads, registers and shelves stay coordinate- and country-keyed so villages, parks and forests can later be new views on the same data, not new databases. What it does NOT license: hoarding, or building database features (bulk pages, advanced search, an API) ahead of demand. It formalises the 2026-08-03 "keep the rejects" ruling and the 2026-08-05 nothing-thrown-away rule as a goal instead of a habit.

- **2026-08-14 — Hidde (session): "Worth the visit?" on every tree, votes stored from day one, shown to nobody yet.** His design: a thumbs pair per tree on city and tree pages ("bijna duimpje op duimpje... dat je top tien daardoor ook slimmer wordt"), where a No opens four chips: not that special / dead or gone / wrong location / couldn't reach it. The first chip is an OPINION and counts as the vote; the other three are FACT REPORTS routed into the submissions table (kind 'feedback') that Step 0b already processes, with the standing same-day rule applying to dead and wrong-location reports. Convention followed rather than invented (Google Maps' rating-versus-suggest-an-edit split, AllTrails' condition chips, prefilled context so the reader never types a city or tree). Three boundaries, all his or ratified by him: **votes influence ranking only when volume makes it honest** (at ~330 visits/week, three votes reshuffling a top ten is noise cosplaying as wisdom; threshold decided when the data exists, and position bias — number one gets seen most, so voted most — must be addressed then); **no public counts yet** for the same reason; **no comments or reviews**, which are moderation and personal data through the back door. The loop it closes: votes → losing tree checked → removed per the bcn_008 same-id precedent → best lead from the shelf takes its place → walk recomputes. The /contribute form stays for whole-city knowledge and now arrives prefilled.

- **2026-08-13 — Hidde (session):** Hard rule 10's ACCESS half is loosened; its withheld-location half is untouched and stays absolute. He opened it with "hoe erg is het nou als een boom op een schoolplein of kerk stad of zelfs bedrijfsterrein, vaak kun je die gewoon gaan bekijken toch", and closed it with "sounds like we can loosen rule 10. agree on the schools unless we find evidence. churches are also fine to walk into right?" What changes: ground type alone no longer blocks a tree. Three tests decide instead (get in without asking anyone's permission, an `access` field that says honestly what the visitor will meet, and not somebody's home), so churchyards, hospital gardens, campuses and open company grounds are publishable with an honest access line, and paid entry is a yes rather than a no. What does not change: private homes and their gardens stay out, enclosed convent and monastery gardens stay out unless a source says otherwise, and school grounds stay out unless there is real evidence the grounds are genuinely open, because that case is about children rather than about the tree. The reasoning that moved it, and it is the transferable part: rule 10 was written about HARM, and "when in doubt, leave it out" was being applied to INCONVENIENCE, where this site's standing answer has always been to say so honestly rather than to hide the thing. Full text in CLAUDE.md hard rule 10.
