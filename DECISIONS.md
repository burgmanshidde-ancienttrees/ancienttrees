# Decisions

One dated entry per decision that shapes the product, newest first: what was decided, by whom, and why. This file exists because the 2026-07-27 assumption audit showed decisions scattered across five documents and chat, which is how observations silently harden into rules. New decisions land here the day they are made; older ones migrate when a document touching them is edited anyway. The standard here is the lightweight ADR (Architecture Decision Record) practice, ours-sized.

- **2026-08-24 - Hidde (session): a routing service may run inside the app, and a reader's tree reaches us whether they offer it or not.** Two rulings the same afternoon, both moving a line the hard rules had held shut.

  **Routing.** "Ik vind die afhankelijkheid van die partij geen probleem voor een betere ervaring." So the app may call a routing service while somebody is using it, which hard rule 5 reserves for him because it is a third party inside the product. Scope as agreed: ONE call per walk, at the moment it is saved, never per tap. A tap that waits for a server feels worse than a straight line, and one call per walk is also what is fair to a community routing instance.

  **Submissions.** "Hij komt uberhaupt automatisch bij ons terecht of ze het willen of niet en dan kiezen wij of die het waard is." The choice between keeping a tree and offering it is gone: one button, and it reaches us; unsigned-in it queues rather than asking. What does NOT move: photographs still stay on the phone, because uploading them is a bucket, a bill and a deletion duty, and that is still his.

  Shipped with it: `/terms`, linked in the footer and under the sign-in button on BOTH surfaces, granting the licence that turns "they agreed" from an assumption into a fact. No lawyer has read it. And published reader photographs carry NO name, which the page states outright as a departure, because comparable apps do credit their contributors and somebody deserves to know that before sending their best picture.

  Also settled the same day, recorded because the tab bar had already been ruled on three times: five slots, Map / Explore / camera / Collection / Profile, with the camera in the MIDDLE and no word under it. That control only exists in a bar of five, which is why the same drawing was an invention that morning and a convention that afternoon: a pattern transfers only with its preconditions.

- **2026-08-20 - Hidde (session): the app's four tabs are Map, Explore, Collect, Profile, and "suggest a tree" is woven through rather than a fifth tab.** *(Superseded the same evening by the Explore/Saved/Spot/Collect/Profile bar above; the free/paid split, the GPS-is-a-bonus rule and the one-free-walk rule in this entry all survive unchanged.)* His own words for each, kept because they are the brief: the map is "gewoon leuk om erheen te scrollen en te kijken" with pulsing blossom and walks as a filter; Explore "is meer inspireren; dat je niet echt weet wat je zoekt, maar gewoon leuke foto's van bomen wilt zien"; Collect is "voor de echte nichegebruikers die het tof vinden om naar bomen toe te wandelen", where you photograph the tree and your GPS proves you were near it, "en daarmee vereeuwig je dat je daar bent"; Profile is everything personal.

  **What it changes about the four verbs** (find, walk, collect, season, settled 2026-07-26 and unchanged as the product): two of them stop being destinations and become controls. **Walk** becomes a filter on the map instead of a tab, and **season** becomes a pulse on the pins instead of a tab. That is not a demotion, it is putting them where they get used, and it is the same move the website made when season left the nav on 2026-07-29. The verbs are untouched; only their furniture moved.

  **What it fixes that nobody had named:** Saved and You were both nouns about storage, and You was doing three jobs at once (the stats, the account and the settings shelf). Collect and Profile split the game from the admin, which is the split AllTrails and Google Maps both make.

  **Three corrections Hidde accepted with "Ok", and the first one is the load-bearing one.**

  **(1) Ticking stays FREE; the PROOF is what is sold.** He proposed Collect as the premium feature outright. That collides with PRODUCT_IA.md's law 3, which is not an opinion but the converged finding of the nine-product benchmark: content is never the paywall, a free account captures the keep-verbs, and the paid tier sells in-the-field convenience. If collecting is paid, a free user has a map and some photographs and nothing to come back for, so the habit never forms and there is nothing to convert. The split that keeps his intent: **free** to tick a tree off, see your own list and your own count; **paid** for the photograph, the GPS seal, the badges and putting your own picture on the tree's page. His own paywall copy of 2026-08-18 already reads that way ("tick off trees you've stood in front of, upload your own photos, and collect badges"); this only says those three need not sit in one bundle. This also resolves the open flag left in `Entitlement.swift` on 2026-08-19, which asked him to rule on exactly this.

  **(2) GPS proximity is a BONUS, never a gate.** Under a dense canopy GPS accuracy falls to 30 to 100 metres, and a veteran tree in a wood is precisely where it is worst, so a proximity check would randomly refuse people who are standing in front of the tree. That is the worst failure this feature could have: the person is there and is being called a liar. So ticking always works, and standing there earns a verified seal.

  **(3) One walk stays free.** The map's "see walking routes" control is a paid filter, per his 2026-08-18 paywall line, with the nearest walk still open, because nobody buys a thing they have never felt. That rule already lives in `Entitlement.swift` as "one real taste".

  **Suggest a tree is woven, not a fifth tab.** A centre plus-tab is the Instagram pattern and it earns its place when creating is the product; here contributing is a rare high-intent act, and a permanent tab would spend the most valuable pixels in the app on something a tiny share of sessions use. The high-intent moments are contextual and already exist: standing at a tree with no photograph, "something here is wrong", and an empty search. AllTrails does the same, with no add tab at all. It gets one fixed home in Profile so it exists when somebody looks for it, and the decision is revisited if submissions ever reach real volume.

  **Not built without a further yes: photo upload.** It stores a person's photograph, which is personal data and storage that costs money, so it needs Hidde on both counts. The rest of Collect is buildable now.

- **2026-08-20 - Hidde (session): a photo credit never sits on top of the tree; on a card it moves to the tree page.** His words, on seeing the app on his own phone: "please dont refer to wikicommons or whatever with an overlay on the tree put it somwhere small on the deeper page." A card exists to make somebody tap it, and a dark chip over the trunk is the one thing on it that is not the tree.

  **What this modifies**, said out loud because it touches a rule he set himself on 2026-07-29 (record always, display when the licence requires it): cards now carry NO credit at all, and the tree page carries it as a small caption under the photograph rather than painted across it. The attribution is moved, never dropped, and it is one tap away on the page the card leads to. That is what Wikipedia's own apps and every image search do with a thumbnail, and CC BY-SA asks for attribution "in any reasonable manner" rather than for a specific position.

  **What does not bend, and is the reason the old comment in TreeCard.swift stays:** a CC BY or BY-SA photograph with no credit ANYWHERE is still a licence breach, and that has already shipped here once. The tree page is now the single place that obligation is met, so anything that renders a photograph without a route to that page needs its own credit. Applies to the app; the website is unchanged.

- **2026-08-20 - Hidde (session): Sign in with Apple is allowed in the iOS app, and the account wall is SOFT.** Two answers given inside one minute, both to the question of how the app gets the most accounts.

  **(1) Sign in with Apple is in.** It needed asking because it is a third party in the product (hard rule 5) and because it needs configuration only he can do. His answer was the recommended option, Apple plus an emailed code, and the reasoning he was shown stands as the record: on a phone, typing an address, leaving for Mail and finding the way back is four chances to give up, and one Face ID tap is none. **What it does not open:** we still store an email address and a collection and nothing else, which is the rule from 2026-08-14 and is untouched. Apple's private relay address is an email address like any other as far as we are concerned.

  **(2) The wall is soft: value first, ask second.** Saving and ticking work immediately with no account, and the ask arrives when there is something to lose. Rejected: a hard wall at the first save, and a sign-up screen in onboarding. The reason, which is the part worth keeping, is that AllTrails can put a wall in front of you because you already came looking for a named trail and they have a million reviews to stand on. We have nine waitlist signups, so the same wall buys a bounce rather than an account. **The moments, implemented in `ios/.../Kit/Nudge.swift`:** the first tick, because a tick is a memory rather than a bookmark, and the third save, because three is when a list starts to look like a collection. At most three asks ever and never twice in a week, because a prompt that keeps returning teaches people to dismiss without reading, and then the one honest warning we ever need to show gets dismissed too.

  Both are built and shipped the same session. The audit that produced the question, and the four dashboard actions that still gate parts of it, are in `UX_AUDIT.md`.

- **2026-08-15 — Hidde (session), the queue is rescored on TRAVEL demand, and the fame penalty it was founded on is withdrawn.** His question: "I don't think Wikipedia impression gives a good representation of English tourist can't we make our top 250 smarter?" He was right, and the answer was testable rather than arguable, because `impressions_10d` from the Search Console readback gives us 55 indexed published cities where we know what our pages actually earn. Measured that day (Spearman against our own impressions): English Wikipedia pageviews **+0.23**, English Wikivoyage pageviews **+0.33**, English share of pageviews **+0.11**. Three things were decided on that evidence. **(1) Demand becomes travel intent:** `scripts/travel_demand.py` fetches English Wikivoyage pageviews via Wikidata sitelinks and writes a `travel` column; `scripts/rescore.py` recomputes score, basis and rank from it. **(2) The fame penalty is withdrawn.** CITY_QUEUE.md was founded on "the more famous a city is, the worse we do there", drawn from ten cities in a digest and paid out as a band multiplying contested cities by 1.08 and quiet ones by 2.50. On all 111 published cities it does not survive: impressions per 100k travel views run 206 / 224 / 184 across demand thirds (flat) and clicks run 12 / 23 / 33 (the other way outright). Predicted yield is now flat: we stopped penalising fame and deliberately did not start rewarding it, because those click counts are small and half the site is unindexed. **(3) The anglophone hypothesis was tested and FAILED, and is recorded so it is not proposed again.** Hidde's instinct was that we needed English-speaking cities; +0.11 is nothing, our six best pages are all non-anglophone, and published anglophone cities earn 1.33 impressions per tree against 1.43 everywhere else, a wash. English speakers do not read in English about where they live, they travel to continental Europe. What did NOT change: `ease`, the four-tree floor, the staircase targets, the never-cuttable list and every hard rule; this changes the ORDER of work only. The known weak point, written into both files rather than hidden: "published, never ranked" still scores 0.25 while 346 pages sit uncrawled, so London, Edinburgh, Portland, Hobart and Quebec City are being demoted for never having been crawled rather than for failing.
