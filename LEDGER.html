What the machine works on, and how far it takes a city

First: open the cities that have no trees yet, to ten, as fast as they go. Set 19 August 2026, reading this very table: a new city taken to ten is a page that can start ranking, while a thirtieth tree in a city we already cover is marginal. Cities with no register behind them count here too; from-zero research is normally off and is on for these because they were named.

Then deepen, to one of three numbers:

new city or village, not yet confirmed by Search Console10
confirmed city20
confirmed big city, 8,000+ travel demand30

Thirty is the ceiling everywhere. A page that needs fifty is a page padded past the point where every entry deserves its spot, and that exclusivity is the product. Every target is a ceiling and a stopping point, never a quota: the moment the next tree gets hard to find, the city is finished for now. Cadiz at five is finished work.

The photo column is the share of that city's trees carrying a photograph, with the count beside it. Trees, photographs and register supply are counted from the project's own data. Impressions and clicks come from Search Console and are a ten-day window, so a zero means the page was not served in that window rather than that it does not exist. Register supply counts designated trees within reach of the city, not trees we publish. Regenerated by scripts/ledger.py whenever the city queue is rebuilt.
