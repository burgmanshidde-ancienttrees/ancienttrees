## 2026-10-31 (session): species answer to every name, web and app

What changed for readers: a species now answers to its name in any of our seven languages, to its Latin, and to English synonyms, and in the app also to one letter off. "Stieleiche", "zomereik", "farnia" or "pedunculte" all find Pedunculate Oak, and the row says which name matched ("Stieleiche · Quercus robur"). Every species row in the app's filter and chooser now shows the Latin under the name. When you name your own tree in the app, species we have met in leads but do not map yet and whole genera are offered under "Not on the map yet", so "haagbeuk" gives European Hornbeam (Carpinus betulus) and "eik" gives Oak (Quercus sp.) instead of a name typed by hand. Why: Hidde, "when adding a tree I often dont know the english name". How: data/species-names.json (scripts/species_names.py, our own translations first, then Wikidata CC0; 586 mapped species, 330 not mapped yet, 200 genera; 40 to 65 percent of mapped species have a name per language), sent as `names` on /api/species.json and as `a` on the search index; one matcher in the app (Kit/SpeciesSearch.swift) used by the map filter, the chooser, map search and the species index. The daily data workflow refreshes it. Check: `check_species_answer_to_every_name()`. Seen in the simulator: the map's species filter with "stieleiche". Not seen rendered: the chooser's "Not on the map yet" section (it needs a tree of your own; unit tested). The app part ships with the next build; the website's search works after this deploy. Not done: names shown in the reader's own language (the app has no translations), and the website's map species filter is still a plain select.

## 2026-10-30, session with Hidde: shares are counted now, and a published photo gets a share

Hidde asked what would make users bring others. The answer: measure first, then the moment people are proud. Before this, no share button counted anything. Now every share link from the app and the web carries `utm_source=share`, with medium app or web and a campaign naming the place: tree, city, walks, map_card, want_to_visit, collect, profile or your_photo. Google Analytics shows those visits as source "share". Each tap is logged as `share_tapped` (PostHog in the app, the events table on the web), and each visit arriving on a shared link as `share_arrived` (events table). On a tree page, once a reader's photo is published, "Share your photo" sits under "Your photo is on this page": a grey button in the app (checked in the simulator), a link beside See it in My trees on the web, in seven languages. The collect payoff's Share this tree moved under Done the same day, after a benchmark showed nobody puts a share between a field and its button.
