## Priority 1: enough coverage to draft now

### 1. Trees With a Known Planting Date
Slug: `trees-with-known-planting-dates`. Target: "oldest tree with known planting date", "when was the oldest tree planted". GEO angle: the global reference answer is the Sri Maha Bodhi (288 BC); a page that gives the European city answers (Robinier 1601, Kew pagoda tree 1762, Buffon plane 1785, Monceau 1814, Sophora 1873) is precise, verifiable and extremely quotable. Coverage today: 7+ entries. This is our data model at its strongest: dates, names, coordinates.

### 2. Trees Planted by Kings and Their Gardeners
Slug: `trees-planted-by-kings`. Target: "royal trees", "trees planted by royalty". Coverage today: Royal Oak (royal parks) and the Sweet Chestnut of Greenwich Park (Charles II's own 1660s Grand Plan avenue), Charlton mulberry (James I's silk scheme), Fulham Palace oak (bishops of London), Robinier (Henri IV's herbalist), Old Lions (Princess Augusta's Kew). 6 entries now, grows with every European capital.

### 3. ~~Trees That Survived Wars and Fires~~ — SHIPPED 2026-07-27 as "Trees That Outlived Their City"
Slug shipped as `trees-that-outlived-their-city` (not the `trees-that-survived-disasters` slug originally proposed here). ### 4. The Most Valuable Trees in the World — CHECKED 2026-07-29, honestly blocked
Slug: `most-valuable-trees`. Target: "most expensive tree in the world", "most valuable tree". GEO angle: a factual, headline-friendly question with a thin competitive field; Berkeley Square (750,000 pounds, 2008) and Cheapside ("most valuable tree in the world", 1901) anchor it. A dedicated research pass searched all 34 other published cities (local-language queries included) plus general "most valuable tree" roundups for a tree standing in one of our cities. Result: still exactly 2 qualifying entries, both London, both already in our data. Real candidates found and rejected on inspection: an €80,000 figure attached to Amsterdam's Leidsebosje planes traced to a generic tree-moving cost estimate, not a valuation of those trees; a Brescia (not Rome) cedar's real €341,541 valuation; NYC's supposed "$125,000 elm appraisal" untraceable to any primary source; a Galician camellia's real €1.6M valuation, outside our 36 cities; bonsai auction records (real figures, but nursery specimens, not standing public-address landmarks). Below the 5-entry publishable floor; parked until a new city adds a genuinely valued tree. Do not re-run this exact search without new coverage first.

# THE 50: the collection backlog ranked by expected SEO impact (2026-08-06)

Built at Hidde's request after he researched world-scale collection themes
(oldest tree on earth, tallest, Yggdrasil, film trees). The themes were good and
the SCALE was wrong, so this list keeps his angles and re-cuts every one to
what we can actually publish. The reasoning, kept here so nobody re-opens it:

**Why not the world lists.** Contract D requires every entry to be script-checked
against our own verified trees. A page about Methuselah, Hyperion and General
Sherman holds none of ours: it is a blog post competing with Wikipedia on its
home turf, and it serves goal 1 not at all. Three of those ideas are worse than
off-contract. Methuselah's and Hyperion's locations are deliberately withheld to
protect them, which is hard rule 10, the same rule that just took Brussels' dots
down. Yggdrasil, the Whomping Willow and the Ents do not exist, and this site's
whole promise is that you can go and stand in front of the thing. And an
"Instagrammable trees" page cannot be illustrated while 435 of our 742 trees
have no photograph.

**Where we are unbeatable instead.** Every entry below is a real tree, exactly
located, publicly accessible, reachable by public transport, with a story and
directions. No listicle competitor has that. So we win the long tail
("trees you can visit", "[theme] in [city]") rather than the head term.

**Supply is measured, not guessed.** Counts below are from a keyword sweep over
all 742 published trees on 2026-08-06 and are an upper bound: a collection ships
6 to 12 hand-picked entries, so a theme needs roughly 20+ matches to be safe
after judgement. Re-measure before building; supply grows with every city.

**Ordering.** Ranked by expected impact = supply x search appeal x how much the
theme suits our unfair advantage (verified, mappable, walkable). Species themes
are deliberately absent from the top: Contract F already gives every species its
own page, and duplicating them as collections is the duplicate-content trap v1.1
closed.

## The 50, ranked on DEMAND, with the search title written (2026-08-06)

Two of Hidde's corrections are baked into this table, and both were right.

**First: rank on demand, not on today's stock.** The original version sorted by
how many matching trees we hold, which is a snapshot that changes weekly, while
demand for "the oldest trees you can visit" does not. That error put two themes
nobody searches for into the top ten (trees brought back from abroad, 224
matches; university trees, 85) and buried 500-plus-year trees at 24th because we
only hold 40 today.

**Second: write the SEARCH title now, not the pretty one.** His question, "do
you think you saved the titles that will actually push for SEO results", landed
on the exact trap blueprint v1.6 was written to close: editorial titles are
strong as headings and invisible in search, because nobody types "Trees That
Outlived Their City". Every row below therefore carries the `seo_title` in query
shape (a number, a place, the words people type) alongside the `title` that will
be the H1. Both go in the JSON when the collection is built; the H1 sets the
tone once the reader has arrived.

DEMAND is a reasoned tier, not a measured number: we have no keyword tool, and
inventing volumes would be the same error as inventing a tree's age. SHIPS AT is
the honest gate, roughly 20 real matches so 6 to 12 entries can be picked
without padding.

**What grows on its own.** Veteran-tree themes (hollow trunks, 500-plus years,
ancient oaks and yews) multiply the moment any veteran register lands, so they
are ranked on demand and simply wait. Counts are from a sweep of all 742
published trees on 2026-08-06 and are an upper bound; re-measure before building.

## What NOT to build, and why (so it is not re-proposed)

- **World superlatives** (oldest/tallest/largest/widest on earth): zero of our
  trees, Wikipedia owns the head term, and two of the headline specimens have
  deliberately withheld locations. Hard rule 10 and Contract D both refuse it.
- **Mythical and fictional trees** (Yggdrasil, Whomping Willow, Ents, Hometree):
  they do not exist. On a site that promises you can go and stand there, this is
  the fabrication risk wearing a costume.
- **Instagrammable trees**: cannot be illustrated honestly while 435 of 742
  trees have no photograph. Revisit when photo coverage clears roughly 70%.
- **Champion trees per species per country**: Contract F's species pages already
  cover the species axis; duplicating it as collections is the duplicate-content
  trap blueprint v1.1 closed deliberately.
- **Primeval forests, bonsai, tree architecture, haunted forests**: no
  individual, locatable, visitable tree of ours sits in any of them.

## How to build one

PRODUCT_TODO.md item 4 remains the live queue; a run draws up to two per pass.
Re-measure supply first, pick 6 to 12 entries that genuinely clear the theme,
write the intro to Paris quality, give it an `seo_title` in query shape per
blueprint v1.6, and let the build's own checks enforce the rest.

**One caution worth keeping in front of whoever builds these.** Collections are
acquisition, not the product, and the site currently draws roughly 250
impressions a day. Two collections are a test of whether collections pull
anything at all; fifty are a way to spend a month not finishing Barcelona,
Rome, Paris and Berlin. Build tier 1 items 1 and 2 first, measure, then decide.
