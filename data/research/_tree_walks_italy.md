# Published tree walks: Italy discovery sweep

Date: 2026-08-05. Scouting only. No trees researched, no city file written, no leads file touched.

**What this is.** Which Italian cities already publish their own tree-by-tree route or
designation list, so the work queue can be built from published routes rather than from
register density. The reason it exists is Setubal: a parish there publishes a walking route
naming each tree with species, place, age and height, and one fetch of it independently
corroborated nine of our ten entries. Registers give scattered trees; a published route
gives a walk, an order and a second source at once. Almost nobody links to these, so they
do not surface in ordinary English searching. Everything below was searched in Italian.

**How to read a block.** "Route exists: YES" means the source names trees INDIVIDUALLY with
a location. A page that tells you to wander a park and admire the old trees is NO, on
purpose: a false positive costs a later run more than a blank does. WEAK means something
real exists but is a tourist article, a newspaper listicle, or a list without locations.

**Standing rule for anything found here.** These are sources to cite and verify against,
never to copy from. No text, no photographs. A licence is noted only where the page states one.

## Milan — YES, and it is the best source found in this sweep

**Publisher:** Comune di Milano, Area Verde e Arredo Urbano, plus the Guardie Ecologiche
Volontarie for the botanical routes. Everything below is on the municipality's own domain.

**1. Thirteen monumental trees, each with its own municipal page.**
https://www.comune.milano.it/argomenti/ambiente-e-animali/gli-alberi-monumentali-a-milano

The index names all thirteen individually and filters them by Municipio (8 in Municipio 1,
3 in Municipio 9, 1 in Municipio 5, 1 in Municipio 8). Named: the monumental bagolaro in
Giardini Montanelli; the plane of Giardini di Villa Belgiojoso (Villa Reale di Palestro);
the 1773 plane at Villa Litta; the plane of Giardino Bazlen; the biodiversity plane at
Giardini Montanelli; La "Pianta" di Affori; the oak of Piazza XXIV Maggio; the "siamese"
plane of Villa Litta; the "grande" plane of Giardini Montanelli; the Caucasian wingnut at
Parco Sempione; the bald cypress of Giardini Montanelli; the bagolaro of Villa Marietti
Radice Fossati; the bagolaro of Giardini di Villa Belgiojoso.

**Depth per tree, checked on "Il platano del 1773 a Villa Litta":** exact street address
(Viale Affori 21), Municipio, a "Dove si trova" map block, the municipal VTA inventory id
(9561), height (32 m), trunk diameter (228 cm), and a dated age derived from a counted
trunk section kept in the villa's Sala Matrimoni: 1773, stated as 249 years. It also
describes the tree's condition (storm-broken west trunk, anchoring cables, a fence against
soil compaction) and cross-links its twin. Last updated 16/06/2026, so it is maintained.
A dated age from a ring count is stronger evidence than most registers ever give.

**2. A city map of the monumental trees.** Each tree page ends with "Dove sono gli alberi
monumentali di Milano? Trovali sulla mappa", so coordinates are obtainable per tree.

**3. Eleven published green itineraries,** index at
https://www.comune.milano.it/aree-tematiche/verde/verde-pubblico/itinerari-nel-verde
(note: WebFetch gets 403 here, curl with a normal user-agent works). Two are true
tree-by-tree botanical routes with downloadable guide AND map PDFs:
- Parco Sempione, alla scoperta degli alberi: two routes laid out by the Guardie
  Ecologiche Volontarie in 2003, 21 labelled plants on the Castello Sforzesco loop alone,
  the second running from viale Gadio past the laghetto. Guide and map PDFs both linked.
- Giardino della Guastalla (the city's oldest public garden), 2005 route, 41 labelled
  trees and shrubs, guide in two PDF parts with the route map.
- A third of the same kind exists in the Giardino di Villa Belgiojoso Bonaparte.
Trees on these routes carry physical labels on the trunk with common name, botanical name,
descriptor and provenance, which means a visitor can confirm a species on the spot.

**Walkable:** yes, and unusually well. Eight of the thirteen monumental trees are in
Municipio 1, the historic centre, and Giardini Montanelli alone holds four of them.
Guastalla, Villa Belgiojoso/Giardini Montanelli and Piazza XXIV Maggio are all one
afternoon. Villa Litta (Affori, Municipio 9) holds two more and is a separate short trip.

**Judgement:** this makes a research pass very cheap. The municipal page is one independent
source with measurements and an address; the national/regional monumental register is the
second for most of them; the Sempione and Guastalla guides add a third for those. Estimate
11 to 13 of the 13 survive a two-source bar, and Giardini Montanelli plus Guastalla is a
ready-made walk. Milan is already published by us, so this is an upgrade-in-place source,
not a new city. Worth doing precisely because the evidence is this good.

**Licence:** none stated on the pages. Cite and verify only; no text or photo reuse.

## Rome — YES, two published itineraries plus a department map

**Publisher:** Roma Capitale, Dipartimento Tutela Ambientale.

**1. "Passeggiate tra gli alberi secolari"**, an actual two-route walking itinerary:
https://www.comune.roma.it/web/it/notizia.page?contentId=NWS1110626
- Route 1, Campidoglio to Via Fabio Massimo: Phytolacca dioica on the Cordonata
  michelangiolesca; Paulownia tomentosa in Piazza della Chiesa Nuova; Washingtonia palms in
  the Piazza Vittorio gardens; Ginkgo biloba at Via Terenzio and Via Fabio Massimo, Prati.
- Route 2, the Gianicolo: podocarp, Himalayan cedar and ginkgo in Villa Sciarra; cedar of
  Lebanon by the Ossario Garibaldino; Phytolacca dioica near the Cola di Rienzo statue on
  the Passeggiata del Gianicolo.
No downloadable PDF; the route is in the page text, ordered, with a place per tree.

**2. "Incontri con gli alberi monumentali, quali sono e dove trovarli"**:
https://www.comune.roma.it/web/it/notizia.page?contentId=NWS1195295
Names roughly 20 trees individually with species and site: ceiba, American oak and cedar of
Lebanon at San Sisto; cedars of Lebanon at the Gianicolo, Villa Pamphili, Villa Borghese,
Villa Torlonia and the Istituto Studi Germanici; Himalayan cedar at the Istituto Studi
Germanici; ginkgo and podocarp at Villa Sciarra; phytolacca at the Campidoglio; horse
chestnut in Via delle Tre Pile; planes at Parco di Porta Capena and the Valle del Graziano
in Villa Borghese; Aleppo pine at Villa Celimontana; holm oak in Villa Borghese; a plane on
Viale Jonio in Montesacro; Californian palms at Piazza Vittorio. It states that the
Dipartimento publishes an interactive map giving each specimen's location, photo, short
description, age and height. Getting that map's URL is one fetch of work for a later run.

**3. The Lazio regional register for Roma Capitale**, as PDFs on the comune's site:
https://www.comune.roma.it/web/it/scheda-servizi.page?contentId=INF208568
Determinazione Regione Lazio G14412 of 03/11/2025 approving the revised regional list, plus
a 199 KB table PDF. This is the register layer, not a route, but it is current to Nov 2025.

**Walkable:** partly. Route 2 (Gianicolo: Villa Sciarra, Ossario Garibaldino, Passeggiata)
is a genuine single afternoon and tight. Route 1 is a city crossing, Campidoglio to Prati
via Piazza Vittorio, more a tram itinerary than a walk. Villa Borghese plus Villa Torlonia
is a natural third cluster.

**Judgement:** cheap pass, and specifically it hands us clusters, which is the thing Rome
currently lacks. Two municipal sources naming the same trees plus the regional register
means most entries clear the two-source bar on their own; estimate 15 or more of the ~20
survive. Rome is already published, so this is upgrade material: the Gianicolo route is a
ready-made walk and worth checking against what our Rome file already holds.

**Licence:** none stated. Cite and verify only.

## Turin — YES, an official 13.9 km urban route with GPX, and we have no Turin page yet

**Publisher:** Turismo Torino e Provincia, the official tourist board, hosting on Outdooractive.

**The route:** "Gli alberi monumentali di Torino: un filo verde nel cuore di Torino"
https://www.outdooractive.com/it/route/percorso-urbano/provincia-di-torino/gli-alberi-monumentali-di-torino-un-filo-verde-nel-cuore-di-torino/804381643/
13.9 km, roughly 4 hours, GPX and KML both downloadable. It is a waypointed urban walk, not
a driving tour, and each waypoint is a named specimen with species, precise site and height:

- Plane, Parco del Valentino at the Fontana dei 12 mesi, 39.5 m
- Plane, Parco del Valentino at the Borgo Medievale, 36 m
- Plane, Parco del Valentino facing the Eridano, 26 m
- Bagolaro (Celtis australis), Parco del Valentino, 23 m
- Four pedunculate oaks (Quercus robur), Parco del Valentino, various heights
- Ginkgo biloba, Giardini Cavour, 27 m
- Plane, Giardini Cavour, 33.5 m
- Plane, Parco Villa Rey, 33.5 m
- Plane, Corso Novara by the Cimitero Monumentale, 27 m
- Caucasian wingnut (Pterocarya fraxinifolia), Giardino Sambuy, 27 m
- Caucasian elm (Zelkova carpinifolia), Giardini Reali Inferiori, 27 m

**A second, independent list of the same trees:** mole24.it (local magazine, so a corroborating
list rather than an authority) https://mole24.it/2025/08/13/alberi-monumentali-di-torino/
gives 16 with the same species, sites and heights, and adds two the route omits: a plane in
Parco della Tesoriera (28 m) and a downy oak (Quercus pubescens) in the Parco della
Rimembranza on the Colle della Maddalena (18 m).

**Dead ends, so a later run does not repeat them:** comune.torino.it/verdepubblico/alberi-monumentali/
has no list, no map and no PDF, only general green-space administration. And MuseoTorino's
"Vecchi e grandi alberi di Torino. Itinerari per cercatori di alberi secolari" (Fusta, Saluzzo,
2013) is a printed book with only a library catalogue entry online, not a usable source.

**Walkable:** genuinely, and it clusters beautifully. Parco del Valentino alone holds eight of
the sixteen, which is a complete afternoon on its own. Giardini Cavour, Giardino Sambuy and the
Giardini Reali Inferiori are a second tight cluster in the historic centre, three trees within
about a kilometre. Villa Rey, Tesoriera, Corso Novara and the Colle della Maddalena are outliers.

**Judgement:** the cheapest new city in this sweep. The tourist board route plus the mole24
list plus the Piedmont regional register at
https://www.regione.piemonte.it/web/temi/ambiente-territorio/foreste/alberi-arboricoltura/alberi-monumentali
gives two independent sources for essentially every tree before any searching starts, and the
heights are already there. Estimate 12 to 14 of 16 survive a two-source bar, comfortably past
the four-tree floor, with two real walking clusters. Ages are the one gap: the sources give
height and species but not age, so that is the work a research pass actually has to do.

## Florence — YES on the list, NO on a route

**Publisher:** Comune di Firenze, Direzione Ambiente.

**The list:** "Piante Monumentali città di Firenze.pdf", linked from
https://ambiente.comune.fi.it/pagina/giardini-e-aree-verdi/il-patrimonio-arboreo
direct: https://ambiente.comune.fi.it/sites/ambiente.comune.fi.it/files/2024-12/Piante%20Monumentali%20citt%C3%A0%20di%20Firenze.pdf
(dated December 2024). The same page links an interactive "Mappa del verde" at
https://ambiente.comune.fi.it/mappa, and the supporting methodology documents: a guide to
assessing monumentality and a circumference threshold table (Tabellacirconferenze05032020.pdf),
which is useful for reading the register's own criteria.

**Also useful:** the municipal census, a first for the city, run with the Università di Firenze
and the Società Toscana di Orticultura, put 19 trees and six tree rows forward for the national
list, with sites given: eight in the Parco delle Cascine, three in Piazza Vasari, three at the
Bobolino, one each at the Argingrosso, Via San Salvatore al Monte, the Giardino dell'Iris, Villa
Favard and Viale Galilei. Reported at https://www.nove.firenze.it/alberi-monumentali-il-comune-di-firenze-candida-19-esemplari.htm

**No walking itinerary exists.** Florence publishes a register and a map, not a route. The Boboli
material found (a roughly 200-year-old stone pine in the upper garden; the early-1800s planes of
the emiciclo between the Isolotto basin and Porta Romana, about 5 m girth and 40 m tall; a cedar
of Lebanon in the lower botanical garden; a ginkgo, a downy oak and a catalpa lower down) comes
from tourism blogs, not from the Giardino di Boboli itself, so it is a lead, not a source.

**Walkable:** the Cascine cluster of eight is a single long park walk. Bobolino plus Piazza
Vasari plus Viale Galilei sits on the Oltrarno hill side and could make a second.

**Judgement:** a cheap pass for verification, a normal-cost pass for the walk, because we would
be assembling the route ourselves. Florence is already published by us, so treat the December
2024 PDF as a check against our existing entries first. Estimate 15 or more of the 19 candidates
clear a two-source bar once the PDF is read against the national list.

## Naples — YES on individually-documented trees, NO on a municipal route

**Publisher:** Regione Campania, Assessorato Agricoltura, "Alberi monumentali della Campania".
Index: https://agricoltura.regione.campania.it/foreste/monum/alberi_monumentali_index.html
Data current to January 2026, page updated 08/06/2026. Each specimen has its own scheda with
photo, description and geolocation, plus an interactive map and a full list.

**Depth per tree, checked on scheda_250, the plane of the Reggia di Capodimonte:** species
(Platanus acerifolia), site described precisely (isolated in a circular plot west of the
pheasantry), GPS coordinates 40.876389 / 14.249167, altitude 125 m, age 200 years, height 25 m,
trunk circumference 170 cm, a photo, and directions on foot from the Porta di Miano entrance.
That is better than most national registers give, and the walking directions are unusual.
Other Naples schede seen: scheda_246 (the Capodimonte agrumeto), scheda_21 and scheda_20 (planes
of the Villa Comunale).

**The clusters are already visible.** 26 monumental trees are recorded for the Campania list in
this area. Five of them stand in the Villa Comunale (three planes, a eucalyptus and a giant
palm; one Platanus orientalis there is 18 m tall with a 428 cm trunk, beside the Fontana dei
leoni). Six more are in the Real Bosco di Capodimonte, among them the Canforo della Regina,
a Bourbon-era camphor of roughly 220 years, about 20 m tall, 730 cm girth. Capodimonte's museum
publishes its own page on them at
https://capodimonte.cultura.gov.it/gli-alberi-monumentali-del-real-bosco-di-capodimonte/
(that host refused both WebFetch and curl from here; a later run should retry it).

**The Orto Botanico di Napoli** publishes a "piante notevoli" route and a georeferenced GIS
census of the Villa Comunale's tree stock at ortobotanico.unina.it/OBN4/mv/ (percorsi/piante_notevoli.htm
and mappa/Villacomunale/VillaComunaleITA.htm). Both refused connection during this sweep and
must be retried; the search result indicates it names specimens individually, including a Cycas
revoluta given by Maria Carolina Bonaparte in 1813, a Parrotia persica, a Melaleuca styphelioides
among the first brought to Italy, and a Camellia japonica claimed as one of Europe's oldest.
Note the Orto Botanico is a paid/ticketed and restricted-hours site, which matters for `access`.

**What does not exist:** no municipal walking itinerary. cosedinapoli.com carries an "alberi
monumentali di Napoli" piece but it is a private culture blog, so a lead only.

**Walkable:** two excellent clusters, each a self-contained afternoon. Villa Comunale (five
trees along one seafront park, plus the Orto Botanico's census of the same park) and Real Bosco
di Capodimonte (six trees in one park, with the museum's own page). These are exactly the
compact walks the clustering doctrine asks for.

**Judgement:** cheap, and the best Italian source in this sweep for coordinates and measurements
rather than routes. Regione Campania is one authority, Capodimonte's museum and the Orto Botanico
are independent seconds for their own trees. Estimate 11 of the 11 cluster trees survive a
two-source bar. Naples is already published by us; this is upgrade material and a possible
regrouping of the page into two real walks.

## Palermo — WEAK

**What exists.** The Regione Siciliana's Elenco regionale degli alberi monumentali, kept by the
Corpo forestale under law 10/2013, at
https://www.regione.sicilia.it/istituzioni/regione/strutture-regionali/assessorato-territorio-ambiente/comando-corpo-forestale-regione-siciliana/ambiente/altri-contenuticorpo-forestale-regione-siciliana/alberi-monumentali-regione-siciliana
307 to 311 specimens region-wide, 75 of them urban, published as PDF and updated to 07/11/2025,
with a scheda di identificazione annex. Palermo province holds the most of any Sicilian province:
133 recorded, 128 in the comune itself, 46 of those in an urban context. That is a register, and
a good one, but it is not a route.

**Why WEAK and not YES on the route question.** The Comune di Palermo's own themed itinerary
"I Luoghi del verde" (https://turismo.comune.palermo.it/palermo-welcome-itinerari-tematici-dettaglio.php?tp=149)
covers 11 green spaces (Foro Italico, Orto Botanico, Parco della Favorita, Parco d'Orleans and
six villas) but names exactly one tree individually, the Ficus macrophylla, and gives it no
location. That is the "wander the park" pattern this file exists to reject.

The one real walking route found, Terradamare's "Radici monumentali"
(https://terradamare.org/itinerario-radici-monumentali-palermo/), is a two-and-a-half hour guided
walk from Villa Giulia through Piano di Sant'Erasmo, the Foro Italico and Palazzo Butera to
Piazza Marina, but it is a commercial guided tour by a tourism cooperative and names only two
trees: a jacaranda whose roots run through a majolica-lined water channel at ground level in
Palazzo Butera, and the Ficus macrophylla of Piazza Marina. Interesting as a lead for one story,
useless as a second source.

**Judgement:** for Palermo, which we have already published, the register is the source and there
is no route to lean on. A pass here costs the normal amount. Note the route it implies anyway:
Villa Giulia and the Orto Botanico sit next to each other and Piazza Marina is a short walk, so
a Kalsa cluster is real even without a published itinerary.
