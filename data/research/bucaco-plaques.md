# Bucaco: the Fundacao's own trail plaques (Trilho das Arvores Notaveis, 2019)

Sent to us by the Fundacao Mata do Bucaco on 2026-08-21 after they had their
biologist check the app. The texts are validated with the University of Aveiro
and the Fundacao, and the trail is registered and approved as a route.

THIS FILE IS A SOURCE, NOT COPY. The plaque wording is quoted here so a run can
verify against it; nothing of it is published verbatim, and the photographs that
came with it are used on the site only by the Fundacao's own sending (see the
photo note on bsc_001, bsc_002 and bsc_010). Written permission with a credit
line is still owed, and drafts/reply-bucaco.md asks for it.

The trail has at least 27 numbered plaques; we were sent the nine that match our
trees. The Santo Elias redwoods have no plaque, which is the biologist's own
reason for asking us to remove them: you cannot get to them.

A WARNING FOR THE NEXT RUN. The Portuguese layer of these PDFs mangles some
digits through its embedded font: the planting year extracts as '1_44' and the
Labillardiere date as '17__'. The ENGLISH layer of the same plaque is clean and
says 'planted around 1644'. Read the English before correcting anything: taking
the mangled digit at face value would have rewritten a correct page.

## Plaque 02
PBH: 2.4 m
> Among the olive trees in the Bussaco Forest, next to the Palace Hotel, there is a particular specimen that general Wellington reportedly tied his horse to, when he stayed the night at the convent on the eve of the battle of Bussaco, on 27 September 1810, now named the Wellington olive tree.

## Plaque 03
PBH: 5.18 m
> There are some large specimens in the Bussaco Forest, including two found next to the guard house on the way to Porta das Ameias [Ameias Gate], and this remarkable specimen, which is about 30 m tall and a perimeter at breast height (PBH) of 5m.
