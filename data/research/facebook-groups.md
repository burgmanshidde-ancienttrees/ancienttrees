# Facebook groups for sharing Ancient Trees (scouted 2026-08-08)

Scouted via search engines only (no Facebook login). Member counts come from public
search snippets and press coverage, so they are approximate and dated; verify on the
group page before posting. 9 groups confirmed real, not 10: see "What did not survive
checking" below. Facebook itself 403s direct fetches; DuckDuckGo and Bing rate-limited
mid-scout, so two counts are marked unknown rather than guessed.

The post concept this list was built for: a personal "I built this, which tree are we
missing?" story. Groups are ranked within each language by fit for exactly that post.

## Notes per group (rules signals and evidence)

- **Monumentale & oude bomen**: snippet reads "Public group · 9.4K members" (an older
  cached snippet said 6.7K, so it is growing). Description: "vooral bedoeld voor het
  plaatsen van foto's (en informatie) van unieke, oude, of markante bomen in Nederland",
  trees outside NL explicitly welcome. A personal I-built-this post with a favourite
  Amsterdam tree photo is on-topic here, not spam.
- **Britain's Ancient and Sacred Trees**: the community's own site describes 100K+
  members worldwide of all faiths and none, scientists included. Woodland Trust
  sensitivities apply: never post exact locations of vulnerable trees there (our own
  hard rule 10 already covers this).
- **Big Tree Seekers**: 200K+ per press; caught Facebook's own marketing attention
  (Times Square ad, 2019). US-leaning but international. The "which tree are we
  missing" framing is native behaviour here.
- **Ik Wandel Graag**: not a tree group, a walking group, but the product's walk verb
  is the pitch, and 200K Dutch walkers is the audience the NL pages serve. Check pinned
  rules for link policy; big Dutch hobby groups often restrict commercial links, and a
  free-no-ads personal project usually passes when posted as a story, not an ad.
- **Unique Trees**: welcome post spells out "Group Focus & Policy: our primary focus is
  original photography of actual trees". Read the full rules before posting; a bare
  link will likely be treated as self-promotion in a group this size.
- **Amici degli alberi**: confirmed real via ADEA (adeaalberi.blogspot.com) which
  credits the group as partner in tree-of-the-year campaigns. Size not visible in any
  snippet; do not quote a number.
- **Natuurfotografie in Nederland**: recent member posts are indexed (e.g. "Mijn 5
  mooiste natuurfoto's van dit jaar"), so it is alive; size unknown.
- **Bäume - einfach nur Bäume!**: found via German tree-community searches; only the
  group URL is verifiable from outside, so treat as a lead, not a booked channel.
- **Tree Identification**: kept as an explicit rejection with the reason, per the
  blocked-leads convention.

## What did not survive checking

- **German and Spanish large groups**: no German or Spanish tree group with a visible
  member count surfaced from outside Facebook. Everything German that looked big
  (Baumpflegeportal, BÄUME sind Freunde, Österreichische Baumfreunde) is a page, not a
  group. Same for Spanish (Árboles Nativos, 58K, is a Buenos Aires page). Pages take
  no member posts, so they are outreach targets for a later lane, not for this post.
- **Ancient Tree Forum, Woodland Trust, Bomenstichting, IVN, KNNV**: all pages or
  organisations, not groups. The ATF page (15.7K likes) could be worth a message to
  the admins one day, but that is hard-rule-4 territory (Hidde sends, not us).
- **A tenth group**: the brief asked for 10; 9 confirmed. The gap is one more
  large Dutch or German group whose member count needs a logged-in check, cheapest
  done by Hidde in 5 minutes inside Facebook search ("bomen", "Bäume" under Groups,
  sorted by size).

## Posting order suggestion (fit x size)

1. Monumentale & oude bomen (perfect topic match, Dutch, his home crowd)
2. Britain's Ancient and Sacred Trees (biggest on-topic English group)
3. Big Tree Seekers (native "find us a tree" culture)
4. Ik Wandel Graag (the walk angle, huge Dutch reach)
5. Unique Trees (only with an original-photo-first post)

One general caution that applies everywhere: post as a person telling a story, one
group at a time over days, not the same text pasted into five groups in an hour;
Facebook's spam detection and group admins both punish the latter.
