# Kauai life check after Hurricane Lowell, 2026-11-03

Hurricane Lowell passed Kauai on 2026-09-08 (gusts to about 90 mph, about 90 percent of power out, many trees down). This pass looked for evidence after that date on every Kauai tree, live or held.

Searched: Kauai County and HDOT storm updates, Civil Beat (2026-09-08), Kauai Now (incl. its 2026-09-09 photo gallery, 2026-09-22 loulu palm piece, 2026-10-29 recovery update), Hawaii News Now road closures (2026-09-08), NTBG's own hurricane page (ntbg.org/stories/hurricane-lowell-update, updated 2026-10-23), a hotel's what-is-open list (as of 2026-11-01), and iNaturalist observations after 2026-09-08 near each pin (any taxon, plus the tree's genus). `scripts/lifecheck.py --file data/research/kauai-verified.json --years 1`: NONE for all three held trees.

## For the publish session: may the held three go?

- **kau_007 Allerton figs**: Lawai gardens (Allerton and McBryde) had "extensive damage from fallen trees and storm debris" (Kauai Now, 2026-09-22); fallen trees photographed in McBryde's upper native section and Limahuli; Allerton House inundated with sand. The figs are not named in any damage report. NTBG wrote on 2026-10-23 that the Kauai gardens are open again, while its FAQ on the same page gives no confirmed reopening date; an iNaturalist observer logged plants inside McBryde and Allerton on 2026-10-20 and 10-21. The $65 tour schedule in the entry predates the storm. Verdict: may go, with the same storm line in `access` and "tours resumed after the storm; check the schedule with NTBG" rather than fixed days, if the pace allows. Not alive-confirmed.
- **kau_008 Poipu Road earpod**: no evidence either way; Poipu was among the hardest hit. Hold until someone sees it, or ship with the storm line if the publish session judges it worth it; a lone roadside tree is the likeliest of the nine to be gone.
- **kau_009 Hongwanji monkeypods**: no evidence either way. Same as the live Koloa trees: may go with the storm line.
