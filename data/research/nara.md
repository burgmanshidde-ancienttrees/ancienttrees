## Shato-no-Ohsugi (社頭の大杉, the Great Cedar of the Shrine Precinct), Kasuga Taisha
- Species: Japanese cedar (Cryptomeria japonica)
- Age claim: 800-1000 years (registry/consensus figure, repeated identically by the shrine itself and Japan's official MLIT multilingual heritage database)
- Location: Kasuga Taisha grounds, beside the Naoraiden Hall, 160 Kasugano-cho, Nara-shi. Precise, named spot (the hall's roof has a purpose-built gap to let the tree's secondary trunk grow through it), not vague.
- Why remarkable: One of the most famous sacred trees in Japan. Appears as a young sapling in the "Kasuga Gongen-genki" picture scroll from 1309, so its growth has been documented for 700+ years. A second, smaller tree ("Shinpaku"/Ibuki, a Chinese juniper) grows from its base and passes straight through a hole cut in the adjacent hall's roof, a living accommodation the shrine still maintains. Kasuga Taisha itself is a UNESCO World Heritage site.
- Sources: https://www.kasugataisha.or.jp/en/guidance_en/modal-23/ (shrine's own official English page), https://www.mlit.go.jp/tagengo-db/H30-00888.html (Japan's official multilingual tourism-resource database, independent government source, matches height/age/girth)
- Verdict: strong candidate. Height/girth given consistently (~23-25m tall, ~8-8.7m girth) across sources.

## Sunazuri-no-Fuji (砂ずりの藤, the Sand-Trailing Wisteria), Kasuga Taisha
- Species: Japanese wisteria (Wisteria floribunda)
- Age claim: 700+ years (registry/consensus figure, matched by the shrine's own site and Japan's official MLIT multilingual tourism database)
- Location: Beyond the Keiga Gate (慶賀門) inside Kasuga Taisha's grounds, 160 Kasugano-cho, Nara-shi. Named, precise spot within the shrine.
- Why remarkable: Its cascading flower clusters hang over a meter long, low enough to brush the sand (hence the name). Traditionally said to have been planted by the Konoe family, descendants of the ruling Fujiwara clan whose crest is wisteria, and it too appears in the 1309 "Kasuga Gongen-genki" scroll alongside the Great Cedar. Blooms early May.
- Sources: https://www.kasugataisha.or.jp/en/guidance_en/modal-24/ (shrine's own official English page), https://www.mlit.go.jp/tagengo-db/en/H30-00884.html (Japan Tourism Agency official multilingual database), corroborated by https://www.japan.travel/en/spot/1008/ (JNTO)
- Verdict: strong candidate. Note: it is a vine/wisteria, not a tree in the strict sense, but the project schema allows visually spectacular specimens and other city pages likely include wisteria as collectible entries; single obvious place to stand (beyond Keiga Gate), so passes the "collectible point" test.

## Great Camphor of Wakamiya Shrine (若宮神社の大楠), Kasuga Taisha precinct
- Species: Camphor (Cinnamomum camphora)
- Age claim: no figure given by either source; girth alone (8m+) indicates a genuinely old specimen, but this is an inference, not a sourced age. Needs a real age/registry figure before shipping.
- Location: before the stone steps up to Wakamiya Shrine (若宮神社), a sub-shrine within the Kasuga Taisha precinct, Kasugano-cho, Nara-shi. Named, precise spot.
- Why remarkable: Actually three camphor trunks fused/grafted together over time into one specimen, 8m+ combined girth, only about 7m tall (likely storm- or wind-damaged crown given the disproportionate girth-to-height ratio, unconfirmed). A named, signposted giant within Japan's most famous lantern shrine.
- Sources: https://www.kyoboku.jp/%E2%91%A8%E5%A5%88%E8%89%AF%E5%85%AC%E5%9C%92-%E6%98%A5%E6%97%A5%E5%B1%B1%E5%8E%9F%E5%A7%8B%E6%9E%97/ (Green Asunara Giant Tree Society, dedicated giant-tree survey group, gives girth/height/location), corroborated by name only in https://wood.jp/8-jumoku/kyoju/fl-nara.html ("Wakamiya Shrine Great Camphor", Nara City) which lists it as a distinct named specimen but with no measurements of its own
- Verdict: needs more verification. Existence and rough size corroborated by two independent tree-inventory sources, but no age figure anywhere and the two sources don't independently confirm the same numbers (wood.jp gives no measurements at all). Worth a session that can also check whether Nara City or Nara Prefecture's own designated-tree list covers it.

## Camphor tree(s) of Tobihino meadow (飛火野のクスノキ), Nara Park
- Species: Camphor (Cinnamomum camphora)
- Age claim: one tree/cluster explicitly dated: planted 1908 (Meiji 41) to commemorate Emperor Meiji's visit to Tobihino, so roughly 118 years old today, three trunks grown together in appearance as one. A second, separate camphor entry in the same meadow ("south end of Tobihino," 7.07m girth, 23m height) is older-looking by girth but carries no planting date.
- Location: Tobihino (飛火野) meadow, Kasugano-cho, Nara-shi, part of Nara Park between Kasuga Taisha's approach and the primeval forest. Named open meadow, not a precise trunk-level pin without a site visit, but a well-known destination in its own right (accessible via Nara Kotsu City Loop bus, "Kasuga Taisha Omote-sando" stop, roughly 10 min walk).
- Why remarkable: A widely photographed single-standing camphor (or fused trio) in an open meadow, historically tied to an imperial visit, visually distinctive against the open grassland (compared locally to Hawaii's monkeypod tree for its broad, balanced canopy). Popular photo subject, easy to find, no ticket needed.
- Sources: https://www.tabicocolo.com/article/0001-tobihino-kusunoki.html (local tourism blog, gives 1908 planting date and the three-trunks-as-one detail), https://www.kyoboku.jp/%E2%91%A8%E5%A5%88%E8%89%AF%E5%85%AC%E5%9C%92-%E6%98%A5%E6%97%A5%E5%B1%B1%E5%8E%9F%E5%A7%8B%E6%9E%97/ (independent giant-tree inventory listing two separate Tobihino camphor entries, "three brother camphor trees" ~140yr estimate and a distinct 7.07m-girth "large hollow" camphor at the meadow's south end)
- Verdict: needs more verification before shipping. Existence, location and general character (Tobihino's iconic camphor(s)) are solid across two independent sources, but the two sources describe what may be two DIFFERENT trees in the same meadow with different girth/age figures, and neither fully agrees with the other on which is "the" famous one. A future pass should resolve which specific tree (or confirm both) before writing a story, and should not silently merge the 1908/three-brothers figure with the 7.07m/23m figure as if they were the same tree.

## Giant Ichii-gashi (evergreen oak), Kasugayama Primeval Forest
- Species: Ichii-gashi (Quercus gilva, an evergreen oak; NOT Ilex integra despite one auto-summary mislabeling it)
- Age claim: no specific age given for this individual tree. General sources confirm the forest itself has been protected as Kasuga Taisha's sacred precinct for over 1,000 years with hunting and logging banned since antiquity, and contains individual giants reputed at 1,000+ years, but that is a forest-level claim, not a verified age for this specific tree.
- Location: along the southern walking trail (南部遊歩道) of Kasugayama Primeval Forest (春日山原始林), a UNESCO World Heritage component immediately behind/east of Kasuga Taisha, Nara-shi. Publicly accessible via marked hiking trails, no ticket. Named trail location, not a precise pin.
- Why remarkable: At 6.00m girth and 35m height, the tallest/largest named specimen on the forest's own inventory; Ichii-gashi (a warm-temperate evergreen oak) once covered the lower slopes here before nagi (Podocarpus) encroached, per a conservation-project source, so a large surviving example is ecologically as well as visually notable. Stands inside one of the very few primeval, never-logged forests this close to a major city.
- Sources: https://www.kyoboku.jp/%E2%91%A8%E5%A5%88%E8%89%AF%E5%85%AC%E5%9C%92-%E6%98%A5%E6%97%A5%E5%B1%B1%E5%8E%9F%E5%A7%8B%E6%9E%97/ (only source with concrete girth/height/location for this specific tree); forest-level corroboration only from https://www.city.nara.lg.jp/site/world-heritage/88532.html and https://kasugatsunagu.com/forest (conservation project site)
- Verdict: needs more verification. Single source for the specific tree's measurements and exact trail position; strong context for plausibility (UNESCO primeval forest, genuinely never logged) but not yet two independent sources on the individual specimen. Worth a second look, ideally at a Japanese-language hiking/nature blog that documents the southern trail.
