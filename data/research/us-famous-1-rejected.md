# US Famous Trees Batch 1 — Rejected/Blocked

## Charter Oak (Hartford, CT) — REJECTED, dead
Fell in a storm on 21 August 1856; a monument (1905) now marks the site. Confirmed by Connecticut Judicial Branch history page and connecticuthistory.org. Not delivered: no living tree exists. Not a candidate for a "living replanting" entry either; no cared-for successor tree with a claimed lineage to it was found in this pass. Source: https://www.jud.ct.gov/lawlib/history/charteroak.htm, https://connecticuthistory.org/the-charter-oak-fell/

## Great Elm (Boston Common) — REJECTED, dead
Toppled in a storm on 15 February 1876, after at least 200 years standing near the Frog Pond. Confirmed by Historic New England collection records and multiple contemporary/historical accounts. Nothing living stands at the site under this name. Source: https://www.historicnewengland.org/explore/collections-access/gusn/198337, https://www.universalhub.com/2017/bostonians-rushed-common-when-they-heard-great-elm

## Senator (tree), Big Tree Park, Longwood FL — REJECTED, dead
~3,500-year-old bald cypress, burned from the inside out on 16 January 2012 (arson, meth-related fire) and collapsed. The standing remains are a ~20-25 ft charred snag, not a living tree. Confirmed by Wikipedia, ABC News, Tampa Bay Times coverage of the arsonist's later conviction. Note for a future pass: Big Tree Park's other named cypress, "Lady Liberty", reportedly still stands nearby and was propagated as a clone of the Senator; not researched in this pass, worth a dedicated lead. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Senator_(tree), https://abcnews.com/blogs/headlines/2012/01/3500-year-old-tree-nicknamed-the-senator-burns-to-the-ground

## Methuselah (Pinus longaeva), Inyo National Forest CA — BLOCKED, location withheld
~4,857-year-old Great Basin bristlecone pine, one of the oldest confirmed non-clonal trees on Earth. The US Forest Service deliberately does not disclose which tree along the Methuselah Trail it is and does not release identifying photographs, specifically to stop visitors touching it or damaging its roots. This is a source deliberately withholding a location under hard rule 10; we do not work the position out from anywhere else (the lat/lng given in the batch file, 37.41598056/-118.16070556, is a widely-circulated approximate grove coordinate, not a tree-level fix, and must not be used as a pin). The Methuselah GROVE / trail itself could be a future lead ("walk the trail among 4000-year-old bristlecones, the exact ancient one kept secret"), but that is a different, ensemble-style entry, not this named tree. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methuselah_(pine_tree)

## Ceiba de Ponce (Parque de la Ceiba, Ponce, Puerto Rico) — REJECTED, dead
~500-year-old Ceiba pentandra, national tree of Puerto Rico, tied to the city's founding legend. Long decline: lost a major limb in 2006 (~30% of foliage), lost another in 2011 (down to ~35% alive), and municipal authorities confirmed the tree dead in 2021. El Nuevo Dia and Primera Hora reported its last remaining branch fell in 2025/2026, after which the municipality cordoned off the remains for safe removal. Nothing living stands at the site under this name. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parque_de_la_Ceiba, https://www.elnuevodia.com/ciencia-ambiente/flora-fauna/notas/historica-ceiba-de-ponce-pierde-su-ultima-rama-tras-anos-de-deterioro/

## Old Arrow Tree (Korbel, Humboldt County, CA) — BLOCKED, access uncertain / likely private land
A large redwood, California Historical Landmark #164 (designated 1935), on Maple Creek Road about 0.7 miles east of Korbel, where travelers historically shot arrows/placed twigs and feathers into the bark to mark a peace agreement between tribes. Wikipedia's own infobox lists its governing body as "private" and notes "there is no official marker at this location" (the physical marker appears to be gone even though the landmark number stands). The surrounding land along Maple Creek Road near Korbel is dominated by Green Diamond Resource Co. timberland, most of which is closed to the general public except by employee/permit access. Could not confirm in this pass whether the tree itself is roadside on public right-of-way or sits on land the public may not enter. Per hard rule 10 ("when in doubt, leave it out"), this is left as a lead rather than delivered: a future pass with time to call the landmark registry or Humboldt County parks, or to check a recent geotagged photo against parcel data, could resolve it. No evidence found either way that the tree itself is dead, so this is an access block, not a death.
