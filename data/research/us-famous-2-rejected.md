# US Famous Trees Batch 2 — Rejected/Blocked

## Thousand Mile Tree (Weber Canyon, UT) — REJECTED/BLOCKED, access not established
The original pine (marking mile 1000 of the transcontinental railroad from Omaha, found Jan 1869) died and was removed September 1900. A replacement pine was planted by Union Pacific in 1982 at the same spot in Wilhelmina's Pass, Weber Canyon, near Henefer/Croydon, Morgan County UT (41°03'11"N 111°31'45"W), and per Wikipedia "stands today within a special fenced enclosure along the original transcontinental line," over 30 feet tall. That is a living replanting, which would otherwise pass under the succeeded-original rule, but no source establishes lawful public access: it sits on or beside active Union Pacific mainline track in a canyon, fenced, with no trail or path described. I-84 passes nearby and Devil's Slide (a different landmark, 1 mile west) has highway parking, but nothing found places the tree within sight or reach of the highway pullouts. Walking active rail right-of-way is trespassing. Blocked on access, not on being alive; a future pass with local/UP contact info establishing a legitimate viewpoint could revive this.
Sources checked: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thousand_Mile_Tree, https://lincolnhighwaynews.com/2008/01/07/the-1000-mile-tree-in-weber-canyon-utah/

## Wizard Tree, Cathedral Woods, Intervale, NH — REJECTED, dead
A distinctive gnarled/serpentine white birch (Betula papyrifera), one of the most photographed trees in NH circa 1900-1904 (Library of Congress photochroms are the only images we have of it). Per the Bartlett Historical Society, cited by the Cow Hampshire blog, it "has not been standing since around 1948"; the 1938 New England Hurricane is the likely cause. Confirmed dead, not delivered. This is a different tree from any other "Wizard Tree" that may exist elsewhere in the US; the batch's photos are all Library of Congress historical photochroms of this Intervale specimen, none recent.
Sources: https://www.cowhampshireblog.com/2014/02/05/new-hampshire-missing-places-the-wizard-tree-of-intervale/

## Webster Sycamore (Webster County, WV) — REJECTED, dead
Once the largest American sycamore (Platanus occidentalis) in West Virginia, ~7.2 km east of Webster Springs on the Back Fork Elk River, 112 ft tall, 25.75 ft circumference. Vandals set a fire in its hollow trunk in 2007; it could not be saved and fell over winter 2009-2010. Confirmed dead, not delivered.
Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Webster_Sycamore, https://www.wvencyclopedia.org/entries/918

## Hooker Oak (Bidwell Park, Chico, CA) — REJECTED, dead
A valley oak (actually two ~325-year-old trees fused into one trunk), once claimed as the largest valley oak in the world, ~100 ft high, 29 ft circumference. Fell in a windstorm on 1 May 1977, estimated at the time to be over 1,000 years old (the fused-trunk age reads differently in different sources; not resolved here since moot). Confirmed dead, not delivered.
Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hooker_Oak, https://www.ijpr.org/show/as-it-was/2014-01-07/giant-oak-once-towered-over-bidwell-park-in-chico-calif

## Vizcaino-Serra Oak (Monterey, CA) — REJECTED, dead
A coast live oak first described in 1602 by explorer Sebastian Vizcaino, standing by a creek in what is now Monterey State Historic Park; tradition holds Junipero Serra celebrated his first Mass in Monterey beneath it in June 1770 (the batch's photos are all 19th/early-20th-century paintings of that mass, not photos of a living tree today). Damaged by lightning in the 1840s, declared dead in 1904 and cut down in 1905. Salvaged limbs were treated with crude oil/creosote and displayed at Carmel Mission and the Royal Presidio Chapel's Heritage Center Museum, but even the treated wood has since decayed; nothing living remains. Confirmed dead, not delivered.
Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vizca%C3%ADno-Serra_Oak, https://carmelmission.org/serra-oak/

## Hanging Tree of Peekskill (Peekskill, NY) — REJECTED, dead
A ~400-year-old oak beside Peekskill High School, site of the 7 Jan 1777 hanging of British spy Daniel Strang, marked with a 1912 plaque by the Cornelia Beekman Chapter, Junior Sons and Daughters of the Revolution. Struck by lightning on 1 June 2006, badly damaged, and removed by the school district afterward. Confirmed dead, not delivered. The batch's only image is a 19th-century engraving; no recent photo exists because there is no recent tree.
Sources: https://westchester.news12.com/landmark-westchester-oak-tree-to-be-cut-down-34903887, https://www.sunjournal.com/2006/07/10/hanging-tree-must-felled/

## Arbutus Oak (Arbutus, MD) — REJECTED, dead
Despite the name, this was a White Oak (Quercus alba), not any Arbutus species; "Arbutus" is the place name (a Baltimore County community), and the batch's genus guess of "Arbutus sp." from the tree's own name was wrong, confirming the caution in this brief. Stood in the southwest corner of the I-695/I-95 interchange ~4 miles SW of Baltimore, seeded around 1690, over 70 ft tall, about 5 ft diameter. Highway planners moved a ramp south in the 1950s to save it, and Native American artifacts were found around it during construction. Split in half and fell in 2019 from internal decay, aged approximately 329 years at death. Confirmed dead, not delivered.
Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arbutus_Oak, https://www.baltimoresun.com/maryland/baltimore-county/catonsville/cng-co-ca-at-arbutus-oak-20190923-akkjrs7xhffdvg237hnrjhekpq-story.html

## Door Tree (Mount Carmel, Hamden, CT) — REJECTED, dead
A local landmark since first photographed around 1898: one tree that had grown into and through another, forming a natural doorway-like arch, featured multiple times in Ripley's "Believe It or Not" and in Rachel Hartley's 1936 History of Hamden. Vandals cut it down with a chainsaw; discovered missing by a Hamden Historical Society supporter and confirmed gone in mid-July 2019 (Patch.com, published 18 Jul 2019). Species and exact felling date not established, but death itself is confirmed by the historical society. Confirmed dead, not delivered.
Sources: https://patch.com/connecticut/hamden/iconic-hamden-landmark-destroyed-vandals-officials
