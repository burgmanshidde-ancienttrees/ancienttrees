## Mingo Oak (Mingo County, WV) — REJECTED, dead
White oak, once the oldest and largest known living white oak in the world (age at death calculated at 577 years, sprouted c.1361), died from fumes off a nearby burning coal refuse pile and was felled 23 September 1938; ~2,000 people watched it fall. Confirmed by Wikipedia and West Virginia Public Broadcasting's history piece. No living successor or clone found in this pass. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mingo_Oak, https://wvpublic.org/september-23-1938-cutting-of-the-mingo-oak/

## Liberty Tree (Boston) — REJECTED, dead
An elm on Boston Common (actually near Washington/Essex Streets, Boston's South End) under which the Sons of Liberty gathered before the American Revolution; felled by Loyalists in August 1775. Already established as dead in our own published Boston page (data/cities/boston.json FAQ: "No. The Liberty Tree ... was felled by Loyalists in August 1775. A plaque and a bas-relief mark the site ... no tree on this list claims its mantle."). A plaque/bas-relief marks the site, not a tree. No living successor claims direct lineage. Not delivered.
