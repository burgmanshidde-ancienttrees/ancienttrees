# Published tree walks and tree-by-tree lists: Spain and Portugal

Scouting sweep, 2026-08-05. Search-language trick applied (ES/CA/GL/EU/PT).
No text or images copied from any source; URLs only.

## Madrid
- Route exists: WEAK (as a *walk*) / YES (as a designation list)
- URL(s):
  - Comunidad de Madrid official Catálogo de Árboles Singulares (1992 decree, 2015 update), transcribed with coordinates and Wikidata ids: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%81rboles_singulares_de_la_Comunidad_de_Madrid
  - Enthusiast site organising that catalogue into visitable routes, incl. two inside Madrid city (Casa de Campo, El Retiro): https://arbolessingularesdelacomunidad.jimdofree.com/rutas/ and per-municipality index https://arbolessingularesdelacomunidad.jimdofree.com/por-municipios/madrid/
  - City council CIEA Casa de Campo leaflet "Principales árboles singulares del parque de la Casa de Campo" (PDF): https://diario.madrid.es/cieacasadecampo/wp-content/uploads/sites/61/2020/03/Principales-%C3%A1rboles-singulares-del-parque-de-la-Casa-de-Campo.pdf  (403 to my fetcher, curl returns an HTML block page; a browser session should get it)
  - Madrid city guided-walk announcements, El Retiro: https://www.madrid.es/portales/munimadrid/es/Inicio/Actualidad/Actividades-y-eventos/Arboles-de-El-Retiro/ and https://www.madrid.es/.../Senda-Las-maravillas-del-Retiro-/
  - Madrid open data, arbolado en parques y zonas verdes (full inventory, not a designation list): https://datos.madrid.es/dataset/300761-0-arbolado-especies
- Publisher: region (Comunidad de Madrid) for the catalogue; city (Ayuntamiento) for the Casa de Campo leaflet and the guided walks; private enthusiast site for the routes.
- Coverage: GOLD-ish but not as a walk. The regional catalogue names 280 individual trees of 56 species across 68 municipalities, each with denomination, scientific name, municipality, coordinates and the reason for cataloguing; roughly 61 of them are inside Madrid city. That is a designation list, tree by tree, with positions. The El Retiro pages are event announcements, not lists: they name two or three trees (Ahuehuete del Parterre, the cypresses at the Palacio de Cristal pond) and no more, so as a source they are near WORTHLESS. The Casa de Campo PDF is the one genuinely walk-shaped city document and I could not open it.
- Walkable: mixed. The regional catalogue is a driving tour across a whole region. Inside the city it decomposes into two real afternoons: El Retiro plus the Real Jardín Botánico next door, and Casa de Campo.
- Official designation list rather than a tourist article? YES for the regional catalogue (legal basis: Ley 2/1991, Decreto 18/1992, updated 2015). NO for the rest.
- Judgement: a research pass on Madrid should start from the regional catalogue, not from searching, and should treat El Retiro and Casa de Campo as two clusters rather than Madrid as one city. Two-source bar is easy here because the catalogue is official and Wikipedia/Wikidata carry per-tree articles; I would expect 10 of 10 to survive, and the real work is choosing which of ~61 make the best two walks. No published municipal route with per-tree detail was found, so the Casa de Campo leaflet is worth one browser fetch by a later run.

  - Plantípodes-AM, a Catalan enthusiast compendium: per-tree fitxes plus a Barcelonès summary with sources and UTM coordinates, 18 pages, updated Nov 2025: https://www.plantipodes-am.cat/fitxes/notesbarcelones/BarcelonesResuminotes2025.pdf and index https://plantipodes-am.cat/notes.html

Each catalogue sheet carries species, catalogue number, location, ownership, height, crown diameter, year catalogued and approximate birth date, plus a description and photo. As a WALK it is weak: the six itineraries covering 50 of the catalogued trees exist in a printed guide (a 2007 UB/Parcs i Jardins publication lineage, and Ricard Llerins' book "100 arbres singulars de Barcelona"), and I found no free downloadable version. Books, so unusable for copying, fine as leads.
- Walkable: the catalogue is spread across the whole city, but it clusters hard: Parc de la Ciutadella, Parc del Laberint d'Horta, Montjuïc, Jardins del Palau de Pedralbes.
- Official designation list rather than a tourist article? YES.
- Judgement: Barcelona needs no more scouting; the register is already in the repo and the city's own sheets are the second source. Plantípodes is the genuinely new find here, because it independently corroborates individual Catalan trees with bibliography and UTM coordinates, which is exactly the expensive part. Expect nearly all shortlisted trees to clear a two-source bar. Value of a future pass is clustering, not discovery.

## Lisbon
- Route exists: WEAK online, YES in print
- URL(s):
  - "Árvores na Cidade: Roteiro das árvores classificadas de Lisboa", 2nd ed. 2018, a published book that is literally a route of Lisbon's classified trees: https://apap.pt/produto/arvores-na-cidade/ (for sale, not online, do not copy)
  - CML "Guia Ilustrado de Vinte e Cinco Árvores de Lisboa", free PDF: https://cidadania.lisboa.pt/fileadmin/atualidade/publicacoes_periodicas/ambiente/Guia_das_25_arvores_de_Lisboa.pdf and Issuu mirror https://issuu.com/camara_municipal_lisboa/docs/livro25_arvores_de_lisboa
  - Municipal arvoredo regulation, defines the municipal-interest classification: https://www.am-lisboa.pt/documentos/1501161034Q8mFK3ex7Ck80HK4.pdf
  - ICNF classified-tree register, national, already imported in this repo: https://geocatalogo.icnf.pt/metadados/arv_class.html and https://dados.gov.pt/en/datasets/srup-arvores-de-interesse-publico/
  - getLISBON, nine named public-interest trees with species and square/street: https://getlisbon.com/discovering/public-interest-tree-in-lisbon/
  - Correio da Manhã data piece with an interactive tree map, filterable by species and location: https://www.cmjornal.pt/DataJournalism/static/arvoresdelisboa/index.html
  - Assembleia Municipal proposals naming specific candidate trees (jacarandas Av. D. Carlos I, the silk-cotton at Campo das Cebolas, tipuanas at Curry Cabral, silk-cotton and camphor at Quinta do Conde d'Arcos): https://www.am-lisboa.pt/503500/1/014654,000155/index.htm
- Publisher: NGO/professional association (APAP) for the roteiro book, city (CML) for the 25-trees guide and the arvoredo regulation, national agency (ICNF) for the register, newspaper for the map, private blog for the nine trees.
- Coverage: the ICNF register is GOLD and we already have it: 69 classified trees inside the municipality of Lisbon, each with species, place and classification. The CML "25 árvores" guide is the WORTHLESS kind for our purpose: it is 25 *species* with example places, not 25 individual trees. getLISBON is small but useful, nine named individuals with exact squares. The Correio da Manhã map names only five outstanding specimens in prose (a ~450-year olive, a 360-year plane, a 38 m date palm, a 140-year Buçaco cedar) but does have an underlying interactive dataset. The APAP book is exactly the Setubal-shaped artefact and is the single best second source for Lisbon, but it is a paid print book, so it is a lead for the shelf, never a fetch.
- Walkable: spread across the city. Lisbon decomposes into clusters, and the register already told us so: Jardim da Estrela, Príncipe Real plus São Bento, Belém/Jerónimos, Campo Grande. Not one afternoon.
- Official designation list rather than a tourist article? YES via ICNF; the city adds its own municipal-interest layer on top, which is a separate and less visible list.
- Judgement: a Lisbon pass is already cheap because the ICNF register is in hand; the marginal find here is the municipal-interest layer, which is NOT in ICNF and covers trees the national register misses. Two-source bar: comfortable for the ICNF 69, harder for anything municipal-only. Expect 10 of 10 to survive if the shortlist stays inside ICNF. Do not repeat this search; the open web has no free Lisbon tree-by-tree route.

## Porto
- Route exists: YES as a programme of walks, NO as a published per-tree document
- URL(s):
  - "Rota das Árvores" 2026 programme, eight guided walks April to November, run by the city with the FUTURO / 100 mil árvores project: https://www.100milarvores.pt/participar/porto-rota-das-arvores-2026 and https://www.porto.pt/pt/noticia/rota-das-arvores-esta-de-regresso-com-novos-percursos-pelo-patrimonio-arboreo-da-cidade
  - City environment department page on Árvores de Interesse Público: https://ambiente.cm-porto.pt/arvoredo/arvores-de-interesse-publico
  - Recent additions, 21 more trees: https://ambiente.cm-porto.pt/destaque/porto-tem-mais-21-arvores-de-interesse-publico
  - Press on the city's intent to build a roteiro of its 230+ monumental trees: https://www.rtp.pt/noticias/pais/camara-do-porto-pretende-criar-roteiro-de-arvores-classificadas_n750341
  - Público, five routes to see Porto's big trees (403 to my fetcher, paywalled): https://www.publico.pt/2017/05/11/local/noticia/cinco-rotas-para-conhecer-as-grandes-arvores-do-porto-1771846
  - National register, already ours: https://dados.gov.pt/en/datasets/arvoredo-de-interesse-publico/
- Publisher: city (Câmara Municipal do Porto, environment department) together with an NGO (FUTURO, Projeto das 100 mil árvores).
- Coverage: this is the most interesting shape found in this sweep and it is NOT a list. Porto has 228 classified trees or classified tree groups (2020 count, isolated trees, massifs and avenues), but the city page does not enumerate them and offers no download; the per-tree data has to come from ICNF, which we already hold. What the Rota das Árvores gives instead is eight named, city-endorsed WALKS: Jardins do Palácio de Cristal + Quinta da Macieirinha + Casa Tait; Boavista and Agramonte cemetery; Passeio Alegre; Quinta de Salgueiros; Bonfim gardens and cemetery; Parque da Asprela; Campo da Cordoaria + Parque das Virtudes; Bonfim romantic gardens. Trees are not named per route in public, and the detailed material is emailed to registered participants only, capped at 25 people.
- Walkable: yes, by construction. Each of the eight is a single afternoon on foot, 2.5 hours, and the city itself chose the groupings.
- Official designation list rather than a tourist article? The 228 count is official; the routes are an official municipal programme rather than a tourist listicle. Neither is a published per-tree list.
- Judgement: Porto is the strongest case in this sweep for the cluster doctrine, because a public authority has already done the clustering and we can copy the SHAPE (not the text). A pass should build Porto's page around two or three of these eight areas, pull the trees from ICNF filtered to those areas, and use the route names only as corroboration that visitors are welcome. Two-source bar: easy, ICNF plus the city. Expect 8 to 10 to survive. The one thing worth a later run is asking whether a route leaflet exists as a PDF; the public web does not have one.

## Seville
- Route exists: NO as a walk, but the best per-tree document found in this whole sweep
- URL(s):
  - "Árboles Singulares de la Ciudad de Sevilla", the full official catalogue PDF, ~390 pages, 6.8 MB: https://www.sevilla.org/servicios/medio-ambiente-parques-jardines/arboles-singulares-de-sevilla/inventario-de-arboles-singulares-de-sevilla_redux.pdf — NOTE: sevilla.org refused my connection; the identical file downloaded fine from the mirror https://www.femas.es/servicios/medio-ambiente-parques-jardines/arboles-singulares-de-sevilla/inventario-de-arboles-singulares-de-sevilla_redux.pdf (verified, opened, fields inspected)
  - Landing page: https://www.sevilla.org/servicios/medio-ambiente-parques-jardines/arboles-singulares-de-sevilla
  - Council announcement of the first inventory, 56 specimens plus 28 tree formations: https://www.sevilla.org/actualidad/noticias/2022/el-ayuntamiento-aprueba-el-primer-inventario-de-arboles-singulares-de-la-ciudad-...
  - Andalusian regional inventory, Seville province volume (Junta de Andalucía): https://www.juntadeandalucia.es/medioambiente/portal/documents/20151/748568/documento_completo_sevilla.pdf and https://www.juntadeandalucia.es/medioambiente/portal/areas-tematicas/medio-forestal/formaciones-forestales/inventario-arboles-y-arboledas-singulares-andalucia
  - Andalusian inventory metadata / geodata: https://portalrediam.cica.es/geonetwork/srv/api/records/c5d37ec9-a857-42b2-a8d2-2e0d4b947a4e
- Publisher: city (Ayuntamiento de Sevilla, Servicio de Parques y Jardines, under the Plan Director del Arbolado) for the city catalogue; region (Junta de Andalucía) for the provincial one.
- Coverage: GOLD, and richer per tree than the Portuguese ICNF register. I opened the PDF and read the ficha template: every entry carries Especie, Nombre común, Familia, tipología (árbol/palmera), etimología, catalogue number and date, Titularidad, gestión actual, Arbomap ID, Emplazamiento, Barrio, Distrito, **Coordenadas WGS84**, then dendrometry (perímetro a 1,30 m, perímetro de base, altura, altura a la primera rama, diámetro de copa medio/N-S/E-O, inclinación) and a condition block (estado fisiológico, fitosanitario, biomecánico). Roughly 340 of the 390 pages are these fichas. It also carries a forward-looking "árboles notables de Sevilla" section, which is a second, wider candidate pool. Named examples in the press coverage: the magnolio of the Cathedral, the "Gran Capitán" eucalyptus, the Chapina ficus, the palms at the Parlamento de Andalucía.
- Walkable: not published as a route, but the Barrio and Distrito fields make clustering trivial, and the obvious clusters are Parque de María Luisa plus Plaza de América, the Alcázar and Cathedral, the Alameda de Hércules, and the Parlamento / Macarena end.
- Official designation list rather than a tourist article? YES, and legally so, adopted by the council in 2022.
- Judgement: Seville is the cheapest Spanish city in this sweep. Coordinates plus girth plus an official designation date means the register alone nearly clears our bar and a second source is a formality for the famous ones. Expect 10 of 10 to survive. The right next step is not more scouting but a parser: pull the fichas out of that PDF into data/registers/seville.json, then cluster with scripts/cluster_register.py. Two operational notes for whoever does it: sevilla.org blocked a plain curl and the femas.es mirror did not, and the PDF is over the 10 MB fetch limit so it must be downloaded and parsed locally, not WebFetched.

## Valencia
- Route exists: YES, five official municipal routes, and they are the closest thing to Setubal found in Spain
- URL(s):
  - The five routes, published by the city's own gardens service: https://jardins.valencia.es/va/blog/descobreix-valencia-traves-de-cinc-rutes-darbres-monumentals
  - Regional Catàleg d'Arbres Monumentals i Singulars de la Comunitat Valenciana (CIEF, Generalitat Valenciana): https://mediambient.gva.es/va/web/cief/cataleg-d-arbres-monumentals-i-singulars
  - The same catalogue as clickable map layer, per-tree popups: https://mediambient.gva.es/va/web/cief/arbres-monumentals-i-singulars/-/asset_publisher/qqRg5ItbO8SD/content/los-arboles-del-catalogo-de-arboles-monumentales-y-singulares-a-un-solo-clic
  - Open data download: https://dadesobertes.gva.es/va/dataset/catalogo-de-arboles-monumentales-y-singulares-de-la-comunitat-valenciana
  - Plantípodes-AM compendium for País Valencià, per-tree notes and sources: https://www.plantipodes-am.cat/fitxes/notespaisvalencia/PaisValenciaResuminotes.pdf
- Publisher: city (Ajuntament de València, Servei de Jardineria Sostenible with the Observatori Municipal de l'Arbre) for the routes; region (Generalitat Valenciana, CIEF) for the catalogue and the open data.
- Coverage: the routes are the GOLD-adjacent kind. Each of the five is a named themed walk through a named set of gardens, and the article names individual trees within them with species and age: "La volta al món" in the Jardins de Vivers (Mexican and Californian palms, Canary pines, Indian laurel, ginkgo, a Chinese fossil tree); "Testimonis de la Història" (medieval wall, Parc de la Cultura, Pont de la Trinitat, Jardins de la Glorieta, Parterre, Museu Benlliure, with a 200-year oak, a Kashmir cypress, a 29 m red eucalyptus, an Australian fig with a 40 m canopy); "Un viatge pel Mediterrani" in the Parc de la Rambleta, built around a 400-year olive; "Un passeig per l'Albereda" (Albereda, Blasco Ibáñez, Jardí de Monforte, with the city's best-known ginkgo and a 300-year olive); "Més enllà dels mars" / "Els indians" (Jardins d'Aiora to the Antigues Atarassanes, casuarina, araucarias, ombú). The city counts 508 monumental and singular trees, 338 generically protected and 169 of local interest; separately it has assessed 41 monumental trees, 254 monumental palms and 167 singular trees. The regional catalogue supplies the hard per-tree data: coordinates, species, popular name, ownership, trunk perimeter, height and crown diameter. No route PDF or map surfaced, only the blog article.
- Walkable: yes, five separate afternoons, each inside one or two gardens. Vivers and the Albereda are adjacent, so those two could merge into one long walk along the Turia.
- Official designation list rather than a tourist article? Both, and separately: the routes are a municipal project, the catalogue is a regional legal register with open data.
- Judgement: Valencia is the best find of this sweep for the walk doctrine specifically, because the city has already published which trees go together and why. A pass should take two of the five routes, most likely "Un passeig per l'Albereda" plus "La volta al món", pull the per-tree data from the GVA catalogue, and write. Two-source bar: comfortable, city route plus regional catalogue is exactly two independent official sources. Expect 10 of 10. Do not spend time hunting a route PDF; check the printed-brochure angle only if a run has spare minutes.

Recommend parking Bilbao until the municipal inventory is published; the one thing worth a later five-minute check is whether bilbao.eus has since released it. Do not repeat the generic search.

- Judgement: the highest-value unknown left in this sweep. If those 187 pages carry per-tree fichas, Zaragoza becomes as cheap as Seville and it already comes with a published route, which no other Spanish city here does except Valencia. The next run should install a real PDF text extractor (this machine has no pdftotext, mutool or qpdf, and pypdf choked on the Seville file's xref) and open both the Zaragoza and Seville PDFs properly. Expect, if the fichas are there, 8 to 10 trees surviving a two-source bar using city book plus Aragón catalogue.

## Cross-cutting notes for the next run
- **Tooling is the bottleneck, not searching.** Two of the best documents found (Seville, 6.8 MB; Zaragoza, 25 MB) are large PDFs. WebFetch caps at 10 MB, pypdf failed on Seville's xref, and this machine has no pdftotext, mutool, qpdf or Quartz bindings. Installing one working PDF text extractor converts two cities from "scout" to "ship".
- **Municipal sites block plain fetchers.** sevilla.org refused the connection outright, barcelona.cat returned 418, publico.pt and diario.madrid.es returned 403. Mirrors work: femas.es served the identical Seville PDF. Try a mirror or a browser session before concluding a document does not exist.
- **The pattern that repeats across Iberia:** the region publishes the designation list with coordinates, the city publishes the walk. Neither links to the other, and the walk is usually the thing nobody indexes. Searching for the region's catalogue is easy; searching for the city's walk needs the local-language route words, which is exactly the Setubal lesson holding up.
- **Plantípodes-AM** (https://plantipodes-am.cat) is a new general-purpose find: an enthusiast compendium of per-tree fitxes with bibliography and UTM coordinates covering Catalonia and País Valencià. Useful as a second source across many Catalan and Valencian cities, not just Barcelona and Valencia.
- **Ranking by how cheap a research pass would now be:** Seville and Valencia cheapest, then Porto and Lisbon (register already ours), then Madrid, then Barcelona (done), then Zaragoza (pending the PDF), then Bilbao (not viable).
