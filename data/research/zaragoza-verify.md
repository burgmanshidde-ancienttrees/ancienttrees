# Zaragoza — verification pass on 9 candidates from "Arboles Singulares de Zaragoza" (Ayuntamiento, 2006)

Task: second source, age evidence, and above all proof each tree is ALIVE 2020-2026.
Book measurements are 2004/2005. Coordinates below are converted from the book's UTM ED50, approximate.

Status legend: ALIVE (dated evidence) / GONE / UNKNOWN.

---

## CRITICAL CONTEXT FOUND FIRST: there is a NEWER official list

The 2006 book is superseded. Zaragoza catalogued trees as **"Árbol Singular Urbano de Zaragoza" in 2019** under the Ordenanza de Protección del Arbolado Urbano. Reported figure: **56 singular trees and 15 conjuntos (groups)** in the city. Finding and reading that 2019/current annex is the single highest-value step for this whole verification job, because inclusion in it = the city confirming the tree still stands.

Ordenanza page: https://www.zaragoza.es/sede/servicio/normativa/4084
Blog transcribing it: http://arbolesdezaragoza.blogspot.com/2016/05/ordenanza-de-proteccion-del-arbolado.html

## Tree 1 — Ficha 54, Almez / Celtis australis, Parque Bruil

**WARNING: there are TWO almeces in Parque Bruil (the book itself says so), and ONE OF THEM IS DEAD.**

- Ayuntamiento de Zaragoza news, **23 December 2017**: "El almez singular del Parque Bruil se convierte en una escultura para homenajear la biodiversidad". The tree **se secó** (dried out / died). Its trunk and main branches were carved by sculptor José Llorente into zoomorphic figures of Aragonese fauna. The article states the tree, at its best, reached **13 m tall and a 16.30 m crown diameter**, and that it stood in the 19th-century gardens of the Juan Bruil estate, near another similar specimen and a singular oak.
  https://www.zaragoza.es/sede/servicio/noticia/226403
- **13 m height is exactly Ficha 54's recorded height (13 m, 2004).** That is a direct match to the DEAD tree. Provisional read: **Ficha 54 is the one that died and is now a sculpture.**
- The OTHER almez is alive and is now the famous one: "Almez del Parque Bruil", **catalogued as Árbol Singular Urbano de Zaragoza in 2019**, fenced in 2017 to stop soil compaction, described as "frondoso y fuerte". Dimensions now: **15 m tall, girth 4.05 m at 1.30 m**. Age: "no hay datos exactos, pero se trata seguramente de árbol centenario" (no exact age, certainly centennial).
  https://www.xn--arbolybosquedelao-uxb.es/almez-del-parque-bruil-zaragoza/
- It placed **third in Spain in Árbol del Año 2026**: https://www.aragondigital.es/articulo/zaragoza/zaragoza-brilla-arbol-ano-2026-almez-parque-bruil-tercero-espana/20251226100528958423.html

**Action: do NOT publish Ficha 54's coordinates as a living tree without resolving which almez is which. The living, famous, nationally-ranked almez is the one to publish, and it is a better entry anyway.** Girth 4.05 m at 1.30 m vs Ficha 54's 2.67 m suggests the living one is the book's "other almez of similar size nearby" — needs its ficha number.

## Tree 2 — Ficha 6, Encina / Quercus ilex, Parque Bruil

- Second source, and it is a rich one: the encina is one of the best-known trees in the city. It stands in what were the Jardines de la Finca Bruil; Juan Bruil hired French gardeners and planted encinas, mulberries and plums alongside exotics (dwarf palms, sequoia, Japanese privet).
  https://www.enjoyzaragoza.es/parque-bruil-zaragoza/
  http://ampacptenerias.blogspot.com/2012/02/la-encina-del-parque-bruil.html (school AMPA campaign, Feb 2012)
  https://biodeluna.wordpress.com/2012/02/15/quedamos-en-la-encina-del-parque-bruil-26-feb-12-00/ ("Quedamos en la encina del parque Bruil", public gathering 26 Feb 2012)
- **Alive: yes as of 2012, and it was the subject of a neighbourhood campaign because the park remodelling damaged its ROOTS.** Sources note it then had no protection plan. Aliveness 2020-2026 not yet confirmed at this point of the pass.
- Age: "centenaria" / "centenario" only. No figure anywhere. Bruil bought the estate and laid out the gardens in the 1860s-70s, which is the real age anchor: if planted with the garden it is ~150 years.

## THE HUERVA RIVERBANK: yes, it has been rebuilt, and it is still being rebuilt now

This is decisive for trees 7 and 8 and I would not publish either without eyes on the ground.

- Ayuntamiento, **8 April 2025**, phase-2 project: **Tramo 2 runs from the Miguel Servet bridge to the Ebro confluence, 1.3 km, 8.4 M euro, 8 months**; Tramo 1 from Puente Blasco del Cacho to Gran Via, 1.2 km, 16 M euro, 15 months. Vegetation management explicitly includes "retirada de ejemplares muertos o dañados y tratamiento de especies invasoras".
  https://www.zaragoza.es/sede/servicio/noticia/339710
- Works on the Miguel Servet-to-Ebro stretch **started November 2024**: https://www.hoyaragon.es/articulo/zaragoza/obras-rio-huerva/20241111115748079217.html
- Ayuntamiento, **February 2026**: **1,371 new trees** being planted along the Huerva banks; **861 of them in the stretch between Calle Miguel Servet and the Ebro confluence**. Species planted include chopo negro, alamo blanco, fresno, olmo, tilo, almez, sabina albar, encina, nogal, serbal.
  https://www.zaragoza.es/sede/servicio/noticia/347687
  https://www.enjoyzaragoza.es/restitucion-paisajistica-del-rio-huerva-zaragoza/

**Read: the exact stretch holding both riverside candidates was a construction site 2024-2026, with a stated policy of removing dead or damaged specimens, followed by mass replanting. Both candidates were already recorded "regular" with dead and broken branches in 2005.** I found no source naming either tree as preserved.

## Tree 3 — Ficha 12, Acacia de tres espinas / Gleditsia triacanthos, Parque Bruil — **GONE**

  https://biodeluna.wordpress.com/2014/11/30/adios-a-la-acacia-catalogada-del-parque-bruil/

### Attrition warning for the whole 2006 book (this is the headline finding)

Same Biodeluna post, citing arborist Ismael Sanz: the 2006 municipal catalogue held **over 65 singular trees, and by 2012 nearly half had disappeared or were in poor condition**, blamed on the absence of per-tree maintenance. Sanz on the Bruil acacia: it was slightly tilted and a prop would have prevented its fall. He also flagged the **Parque Bruil encina as at serious risk and needing urgent support** at that date.

**So: assume nothing in the 2006 book survives. Every tree needs positive, dated proof of life before it ships.** So far the book's Parque Bruil trio is 1 dead (almez ficha 54, 2017), 1 fallen (gleditsia ficha 12, winter 2014), 1 at risk (encina).

## THE LEAD TREE: the LIVING Almez / Litonero del Parque Bruil (not Ficha 54)

**ALIVE: YES, confirmed December 2025.** This is the strongest-evidenced tree in Zaragoza and it should be the entry.

- **Third in Spain, Arbol del Ano 2026**, with **1,799 votes**, behind the Tilo y Tila de El Arenal (Avila, 6,910 votes, winner) and the Eucalipto Rojo de Paiporta (Valencia). Result announced **25-26 December 2025**.
  https://www.ultimahora.es/noticias/comunidades/2025/12/25/2538449/almez-del-parque-bruil-zaragoza-logra-tercer-puesto-concurso-arbol-del-ano-2026.html

  https://www.aragondigital.es/articulo/zaragoza/zaragoza-brilla-arbol-ano-2026-almez-parque-bruil-tercero-espana/20251226100528958423.html (403 to bots, but indexed)
- Candidacy announced Nov 2025: https://arainfo.org/el-almez-del-parque-bruil-de-zaragoza-sera-uno-de-los-candidatos-al-concurso-arbol-del-ano-2026/ ; https://www.hoyaragon.es/articulo/zaragoza/almez-bruil-arbol/20251110130109115555.html ; https://www.enjoyzaragoza.es/arbol-del-ano-espana
- Official competition page (Bosques Sin Fronteras / Arbol y Bosque del Ano): https://www.xn--arbolybosquedelao-uxb.es/almez-del-parque-bruil-zaragoza/
- **Current dimensions: 15 m tall, girth 4.05 m at 1.30 m.** Date of measurement not stated, but published with the 2025-26 candidacy.
- **Age: "no hay datos exactos, pero se trata seguramente de arbol centenario."** No firmer figure exists anywhere I could find. The real anchor is the estate: the tree stood in the gardens Juan Bruil laid out in the 19th century, which puts a planted specimen at roughly 150 years.
- **Catalogued as "Arbol Singular Urbano de Zaragoza" in 2019.**
- The story, which is a genuinely good one and is well sourced: in **2015** neighbourhood associations and ecologist groups asked for a specialist visit because the tree was stressed by soil compaction from foot traffic; in **2017** the Ayuntamiento fenced it; it now "luce frondoso y fuerte". The 2026 candidacy was pushed by residents of San Agustin, Tenerias, La Madalena, Las Fuentes and San Miguel.
- The competition page itself refers to **"el viejo almez" as the companion they could not save** — that is Ficha 54, the one carved into a sculpture in Dec 2017. Confirms the pair and which one died.

## Tree 5 — Ficha 52, Tejo / Taxus baccata, Paraninfo Universitario

- **ALIVE: UNKNOWN.** No source of any date confirms the yew still stands. The building itself is exhaustively documented (BIC 1978, Ricardo Magdalena, inaugurated 1893, fully restored 2008) and not one of those sources mentions its trees.
- Second source: none found for the tree. Only the Ordenanza's Anejo III lists trees "en el recinto del Paraninfo Universitario" (owner: Universidad de Zaragoza), which is the same 2005/6 catalogue re-published, not independent.
- Age: none anywhere.
- **The old botanical garden claim does NOT check out as stated.** The historic Jardin Botanico de Zaragoza was founded **1796** by the Real Sociedad Economica Aragonesa on land in the **Huerta de Santa Catalina, entered from Calle San Miguel 30**, wrecked when the French breached the walls in the Sitios, later rebuilt. The garden that exists today is a different, 20th-century one: designed by Javier de Winthuysen, inaugurated **1927**, in Parque Grande Jose Antonio Labordeta, reopened December 2025 as the Jardin Botanico "Francisco Loscos".
  https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jard%C3%ADn_bot%C3%A1nico_de_Zaragoza
  https://www.zaragoza.es/sede/servicio/noticia/346027
  Calle San Miguel 30 is a few hundred metres from Plaza Basilio Paraiso but it is **not** the Paraninfo site. I could not verify that the 18th-century garden ever occupied the Paraninfo plot, and therefore could not verify that these yews are survivors of it. **Treat the book's "perhaps survivors of the old botanical garden" as an unverified local tradition and do not repeat it as fact.**
- **Access: not established.** The Paraninfo is a public cultural building (library, Museo de Ciencias Naturales, events) at Plaza Basilio Paraiso 4, entered up steps from the plaza. Whether its garden/recinto is freely walkable, and whether the yews stand in a courtyard or in the strip facing the plaza, I could not determine from any source. https://paraninfo.unizar.es/ubicacion

## Extra finding relevant to the robinia (tree 8)

**Robinia pseudoacacia is on Spain's Catalogo/Listado de Especies Exoticas Invasoras**, and the Huerva phase-1 works explicitly included "tratamiento de especies invasoras". A designated-but-invasive riverside robinia in "regular" condition, in a bank that has just been rebuilt, is about as likely to have survived as anything gets. Combine with the Confederacion Hidrografica del Ebro being its owner rather than the city.
