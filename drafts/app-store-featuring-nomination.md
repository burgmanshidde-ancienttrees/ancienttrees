# App Store featuring nomination: the answers, ready to paste

Hidde raised this on 2026-09-04: one form, free distribution, and nobody in
this niche does it. He is right that it is free and unusually cheap for what
it can return, and it is his to submit, not mine: it goes through his App
Store Connect account and it speaks as him (hard rule 4).

**Where it lives.** App Store Connect, then the app, then the left-hand menu:
"Featuring Nominations", then the plus button. It is one form per nomination
and you may file more than one over time. Apple asks for it roughly six to
eight weeks before whatever moment you are nominating for.

**What Apple actually asks for**, and the answer for each. Every field below is
a draft in your voice; change anything that does not sound like you.

---

**Nomination type.** New app, or a moment. Pick "App launch" for the first
one.

**When should this be considered.** Put the launch week here. If you would
rather aim at a moment, the honest one for this app is spring: the season
people go outside and the season half our trees are at their best.

**Name and one-line description.**

> Ancient Trees. It maps the remarkable old trees of the world's great cities,
> up to ten per city, and takes you to them.

**What makes it worth featuring.** Apple asks what is special, and what they
mean is: what would we show on a card. Three sentences, no more.

> Every tree here was verified by hand against at least two independent
> sources, and every pin says honestly whether it marks the trunk or only the
> area. There are 2,469 of them across 203 places, from a 2,000-year-old oak
> in a Danish forest to a ginkgo that is the first tree Seoul ever protected.
> Nobody has made this: the tree registers of the world are government
> spreadsheets, and the one site that collects them is unusable on a phone.

**Which technologies does it use.** Only tick what is true. Today that is
SwiftUI, MapKit or MapLibre for the map, Core Location, and the widget if one
ships by then. Do not tick anything aspirational; Apple checks.

**Design and craft.** They ask this separately and it is where our answer is
strongest.

> The whole app is built around one promise: a good afternoon outside, looking
> up at something old. So the map leads, the walks string trees together, and
> a tree page tells you why this one is worth the walk rather than listing its
> measurements. Where we do not know something, the page says so and asks the
> reader, rather than filling the gap with a guess.

**Accessibility.** Answer honestly. What is true today: Dynamic Type
throughout, VoiceOver labels on every control, and 44 pt targets enforced by
our own build gate. Do not claim anything beyond that.

**Localisations.** Seven languages on the website (Spanish, Italian, Dutch,
German, Portuguese, French, Japanese); say which of those the app ships with
at the time you file.

**Links.** ancienttrees.app, and the App Store page once it exists.

---

**Two things worth doing before you file**, both cheap:

1. **Screenshots.** The nomination is read next to them. CONVENTIONS.md
   already records the App Store screenshot convention from 2026-08-29.
2. **A single sentence you can repeat.** The one above ("it maps the
   remarkable old trees of the world's great cities and takes you to them") is
   the one I would use everywhere: the form, the App Store subtitle, and any
   press mail.

from here, change what does not sound like you, and send.
