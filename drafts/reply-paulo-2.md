**What I checked before writing, because his own caveat needed it.** He wrote
that only the ginkgo photograph on that page is his, and that the other text and
the photographs illustrating it are not. That is a real distinction and it lands
exactly on our photo: he has written about this tree twice.

- The photograph we publish, `photos1.blogger.com/img/291/1346/480/Ginkgo-amarela.jpg`,
  is the lead image of the post "Alerta amarelo" (5 December 2005), published by
  Paulo Araújo, and the only photo credit on that page is "Fotos: © Paulo V.
  Araújo". So the photo now live is his to grant, and his yes covers it.
- The OTHER post about the same tree, "Golden time - Ginkgo" (December 2004),
  carries the credit "Fotos: manueladlramos-0412". Those photographs are not
  his, we do not use them, and no future photo pass may take them. Recorded in
  CURATION.md so this cannot be rediscovered the expensive way.

So there is nothing to ask him about the licence, which is the point: the answer
existed on his own blog and asking would have spent his goodwill on a question I
could settle myself.

**The one question in the mail is a tree, per the standing rule.** A commenter
on that same 2005 post, in January 2008, argues that a finer ginkgo stands in
Braga, in the garden of Casa do Passadiço, opposite the Frigideiras do Cantinho.
Braga is not on the map (rank 167, target 10) and the ICNF register holds 51
classified entries in that district, so the city is cheap to open when its turn
comes. The lead is on the shelf at `data/leads/braga.json` with the thing that
will decide it: Casa do Passadiço is a private house, so unless that garden is
genuinely open to visitors, hard rule 10 says the tree never ships however fine
it is. Not a word of that belongs in the mail; it is one question to a man who
knows Portuguese trees.
