# FOR HIDDE: reply to Vera Donata Wesinger, Bayerische Schlösserverwaltung

**The referral worked.** We wrote to the Englischer Garten administration in
batch 004 and they passed it to the Gartenreferentin, which is the fourth time
a forward has produced more than the original address would have.

**She offers photographs of the old trees in BOTH the Englischer Garten and
Schlosspark Nymphenburg, mid to late September.** Our gap there is smaller than
she probably assumes: of her twelve trees on our pages, three have no
photograph. Munich as a whole is 30 of 44 without one, so if the offer is wider
than those two parks it is worth far more. The mail names which three, because a specific list is easier to say yes to
than "photographs please": the lime and the small-leaved lime in the
Englischer Garten (muc_020, muc_022) and the weeping beech at the
Schwanenbrücke in Nymphenburg (muc_014). My first draft named a copper beech
that does not exist there, which is exactly the kind of thing that makes a
custodian doubt the rest of the page.

**Her correction is right and is now live.** Our line read "Most of the
Englischer Garten's protected trees are old because they survived", which
makes it sound like luck. Her point: they survived because the park is a
protected Gartendenkmal, has been cared for by the Schlösserverwaltung for over
two hundred years, and lies in a Landschaftsschutzgebiet, and that combination
held off the building interests of the city growing around it. That is a better
sentence than ours and it names a mechanism rather than an accident. The
custodian of the ground is the better source on why the ground still exists,
and her reasoning is recorded in the tree's verify_notes.

No ask beyond confirming the photographs, and deliberately no mention of a
link: she has offered something concrete and September is the moment to be easy
to deal with.
