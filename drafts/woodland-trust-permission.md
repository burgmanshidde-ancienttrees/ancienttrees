# FOR HIDDE: draft email to the Woodland Trust

To: ancienttreeinventory@woodlandtrust.org.uk
Subject: Permission request: showing ATI trees on ancienttrees.app

Draft below, written to be sent from your own address. Facts in it are
accurate as of 2026-08-06; adjust the tone as you like. The one thing not to
soften is the honesty about a possible paid tier later: their licence defines
commercial as including indirect and deferred gain, so vagueness there would
get a permission that does not cover us.

---

Dear Ancient Tree Inventory team,

I love old trees. Standing in front of something that has quietly outlived
every generation that planted, pruned or ignored it is, for me, one of the
best reasons to go outside at all. I built ancienttrees.app to share that
feeling: it maps remarkable ancient trees in cities, tells each one's story
in a few honest paragraphs, and gives people a short walk that strings a few
together. The goal is simply to get more people outdoors, looking up at
something old, and caring about what they find there. Around 90 cities are
live today, about 740 trees, each verified against at least two sources.

I would like to ask for written permission to use Ancient Tree Inventory data
on the site, which your End User Licence Agreement asks me to do, since the
site is a publicly available internet mapping service.

Specifically, I would like to show a selection of ATI-recorded ancient and
veteran trees in and around UK cities (starting with London) as points on the
site's map, labelled as coming from the Ancient Tree Inventory, with the
attribution wording your licence specifies and a citation with access date.
Two commitments up front, because they match how the site already treats
every source:

- I would only show trees that are publicly accessible, and I would honour
  every location your inventory obscures or withholds. The site's standing
  rule is that a source hiding a tree's position knows the tree better than
  we do, and we never undo it.
- The site is free to use today. I want to be straightforward that it may
  eventually have a paid tier (for features like routes and offline maps,
  not for access to the tree data itself). If that possibility affects what
  permission you can give, I would rather know now, and I am happy to
  discuss terms that work for the Trust, including a licence fee if that is
  the appropriate route.

The Inventory is by far the most complete record of the UK's ancient trees,
and the volunteer work behind it deserves to reach people who would love
these trees. I would be glad to link prominently to the ATI so visitors can
explore the full inventory and contribute records themselves.

Thank you for considering it.

Best regards,
Hidde Burgmans
ancienttrees.app
