Hi Kate,

Thanks for coming back to me so quickly. www.ancienttrees.app is live now as a
website, and I am working on an iOS and Android app at the moment while
gathering data.

Ancienttrees.app maps remarkable ancient trees city by city, tells the story of
each one, and strings a few into a short walk you can do in an afternoon. There
are 95 cities and 893 trees live right now, 8 of those cities in the UK.

It is just me. No institution, no funding, and it earns nothing. I am a
designer in the Netherlands who likes old trees. A paid tier is possible later
for things like routes and offline maps, never for the tree records themselves.

On records going stale. I would not be publishing a copy of the Inventory.
Every tree on the site is verified against at least two independent sources and
gets its own written story. An ATI record would be one of those sources and the
start of a search, not the end of it. For London that means a handful of trees,
not the inventory.

What I would commit to:

- Nothing goes live unverified. I check that a tree is still standing, and
  where exactly, before publishing it.
- Dead trees come off the site rather than into an archive. Every tree page
  asks readers to report problems, and anything reported gone gets checked and
  taken down the same day.
- Every pin says whether it is exact or approximate.
- Attribution and a link back to the ATI record on every tree that came from it.
- I would remove or correct anything you ask, no questions.

It can work both ways. Readers write in, often while standing in front of the
tree. If someone tells me one has come down, I would pass that back to you.

Happy to start small, London only, a handful of trees, so you can see how it is
handled first. And if it is a no, I would rather hear that than have it go
quiet.

Thanks for taking it to your colleagues.

Best,
Hidde
