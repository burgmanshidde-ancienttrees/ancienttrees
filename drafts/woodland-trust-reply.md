Dear Kate,

Thank you for coming back to me, and for the honest question about records
going stale. That is the right thing to worry about and I would rather answer
it properly than briefly.

First, to be precise about what it is, since I called it an app and that was
sloppy of me. Today it is a website, ancienttrees.app. It maps remarkable
ancient trees city by city, tells the story of each one, and strings a few
into a short walk you can do in an afternoon. There are 95 cities and 893
trees live now, 8 of those cities in the UK. An iOS app is what I would like
to build next, but it does not exist yet.

I am a private individual. There is no institution behind it, no funding, and
it earns nothing. I am a designer in the Netherlands who likes old trees and
wanted the thing to exist. The site is live and free, and as I said, a paid
tier is possible later for features like routes and offline maps, never for
access to the tree records themselves.

On keeping records current, which I think is the question that actually
decides this.

I would not be taking a copy of the Inventory and publishing it. What this
site does is much closer to what you describe as processing data into
something else. Every tree that gets published here is verified individually
against at least two independent sources before it goes live, and it gets a
written story of its own. A record from the ATI would be one of those
sources, and the starting point for a search rather than the end of one. For
London that would mean a handful of trees, not the inventory.

Concretely, what I would commit to:

- Nothing goes live unverified. An ATI record tells me a tree is worth
  looking at. Whether it is still standing, and where exactly, I check
  myself before publishing.
- A tree that has died is removed, not archived. The whole point of the site
  is standing in front of something that has been alive for centuries, so a
  dead tree breaks my product before it embarrasses yours. Every tree page
  asks readers to tell us if something is wrong, and anything reported gone
  gets checked and taken down the same day.
- Locations are labelled honestly. Every pin says whether it is exact or
  approximate, because sending someone to a spot where the tree is not is the
  one mistake I cannot afford.
- I would re-check the published UK trees against the Inventory on a fixed
  schedule, and remove or correct anything the Trust asks me to, without
  argument and without needing a reason.
- Attribution and a link back to the ATI record on every tree that came from
  it, so people can see the source and add records themselves.

There is also something in this for the Inventory, if you want it. Readers do
write in, and they are often standing in front of the tree when they do. If
someone tells me a tree has come down or that a record is wrong, I would be
glad to pass that back to you rather than only fixing it on my side. Small,
but it points the right way.

I am happy to start with something deliberately small, London only, a limited
number of trees, so you can see how it is handled before anything wider. And
if the answer is that this is not something the Trust wants to do, I would
rather hear that than have it go quiet.

Thank you for discussing it with your colleagues.
