# Content apps started small, dense and by hand

Research report, 2026-10-23. Built from four research notes on nine apps (AllTrails, komoot, Wikiloc, Strava, Untappd, Polarsteps, iNaturalist, Geocaching, Atlas Obscura) plus the general cold-start literature (Andrew Chen, Lenny Rachitsky, Paul Graham) and the record on Google demotion recoveries. Where the notes mark a claim as secondhand, conflicting or unverified, this report says so.

**None of the nine apps got its first thousand users from a channel mix, a marketing budget or app-store search; each got them from one pre-existing gathering place where people already held the content, and the content was either imported before anyone arrived (AllTrails, komoot) or produced as a by-product of something the contributor was doing for themselves (Strava, Wikiloc, Untappd, Polarsteps, Geocaching, iNaturalist).** The first contributors were in every documented case a handful of people the founders knew or hand-picked, and in the three hand-seeded catalogues (Atlas Obscura, Geocaching, iNaturalist) the founders themselves wrote the first hundred entries. Ambassadors, badges and events show up only after the first thousand, with two exceptions (Untappd's badges from day one, Atlas Obscura's Obscura Day nine months in). Timing ran in years, not weeks: Strava took about three years to reach 20,000 users, iNaturalist ran flat for three years before an institutional partner "brought the party", Polarsteps had a few hundred users seven months after launch. Across Lenny Rachitsky's 100+ consumer companies, going where the audience already gathers was the most common and highest-ROI first-1,000 tactic (over 50 percent), nobody succeeded with more than three channels, and SEO, waitlists and app stores barely figure ([Lenny's Newsletter](https://lennysnewsletter.com/i/38506758/calm)). For Ancient Trees this means the catalogue of 3,800 trees is the seeding step already done at AllTrails scale, the missing step is one dense place with a handful of hand-recruited photographers, and the next six weeks should go to that one place and the two or three gathering places where old-tree photographers already are, not to broader posting, new cities or Google polishing. The thinnest parts of the evidence are the exact first-user numbers for Strava and Untappd, komoot's early record (nearly empty), and the complete absence of any documented solo-maker, place-based photo app that went from hundreds to thousands of contributors.

## Three starting shapes, and Ancient Trees has already done the first one

The nine apps fall into three shapes by where the day-one content came from. The first shape is the import. **AllTrails launched in December 2010 on public US government data (US Forest Service, National Park Service) and had 45,000 North American trails catalogued by November 2011**, before any community existed; its 2012 description of "crowd-sourced" meant user edits to that base checked by a team of editors who "sometimes call the park" ([TechCrunch 2011](https://techcrunch.com/2011/11/13/alltrails-pockets-400k-from-500-startups-and-more/); [TechCrunch 2012](https://techcrunch.com/2012/01/17/alltrails-partners-with-national-geographic-launches-redesigned-co-branded-website)). komoot went further and never needed seeded content at all: its 2010 product computed routes from OpenStreetMap with the GraphHopper router, so a useful route existed in any region with zero users, and community Highlights were an additive layer documented only by September 2014 ([de.wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Komoot); [PR Newswire 2014](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/komoot-launches-in-the-us-to-inspire-your-outdoor-experiences-on-heels-of-becoming-europes-leading-inspiration-app-277079101.html)). In both cases the user layer rode on a catalogue that was complete first, and the catalogue, not the community, caught the early search traffic.

The second shape is the hand-written seed, a few dozen to a hundred items, followed by an open ask. Atlas Obscura's founders "added the first hundred or so places themselves", launched in June 2009 through a week of guest posts on Boing Boing with the explicit line that they were counting on readers to fill out the map, and showed newcomers the founders' entries as templates ([Get Together case study](https://gettogether.substack.com/p/the-national-geographic-of-the-digital); [Boing Boing 2009](https://boingboing.net/2009/06/15/introducing-the-atla.html)). **Geocaching.com opened on 2 September 2000 with 75 caches typed in by hand from e-mails**, and the founder said he wanted a bigger community "since he couldn't hide and find them all himself" ([Geocaching HQ podcast transcript](https://www.geocaching.com/blog/?p=66865)). iNaturalist's observation 2, the lowest-numbered record still on the site, is a bulrush growing in the founder's Berkeley office in March 2008 ([iNaturalist Forum](https://forum.inaturalist.org/t/first-records-submitted-to-inat/70413)). This is the "flintstoning" Andrew Chen describes, manual founder labour standing in for the side of the network that does not yet exist, with Reddit's fake accounts as the dishonest version and Yelp and Quora named alongside it ([Goodreads highlights of The Cold Start Problem](https://www.goodreads.com/notes/56962799-the-cold-start-problem/10957646-claus-enevoldsen?page=2); [Vice on Reddit's fake accounts](https://www.vice.com/en/article/how-reddit-got-huge-tons-of-fake-accounts-2/)).

The third shape is the personal log that happens to be shareable. Wikiloc began as the founder's own weekend mountain-bike routes with photos, built for a riding group that said "yo también quiero" ([El Español](https://www.elespanol.com/omicrono/tecnologia/20210605/wikiloc-espanola-triunfa-rutas-imposibles-empezo-hobby/586442340_0.html)). Strava's 2008 MVP was "just a leaderboard that tracked the efforts of a few athletes across a couple of Vermont and California cycling segments" ([Contrary Research](https://research.contrary.com/company/strava)). Polarsteps started as one sailor's daily satellite-phone coordinate plotted as a dot on a map for family at home ([Job Harmsen, LinkedIn](https://nl.linkedin.com/in/jobharmsen)). Untappd's September 2010 private beta was a Foursquare-style beer check-in with no photos, no ratings and no moderation ([Untappd blog](https://untappd.com/blog/cheers-to-11-years-of-check-ins/1356)). In this shape the contributor never writes for strangers; the shared content costs nothing extra because it is the log itself.

Ancient Trees sits squarely in shape one. The 3,800 machine-written pages are the AllTrails import done honestly, and the project has already met the import shape's documented failure: a catalogue that readers consult without contributing is exactly what Chen calls being "stuck on just the tool" ([Goodreads highlights](https://www.goodreads.com/notes/56962799-the-cold-start-problem/10957646-claus-enevoldsen?page=2)). AllTrails' own account of its contribution flywheel is instructive about timing: its growth lead, who joined in 2015 as the sixth employee, says the data was bad and the product "sucked" until the native apps began recording hikes "around 2015", at which point every walk became passive data and ratings, photos and reviews rode on top of it ([Sub Club podcast](https://subclub.com/episode/ron-schneidermann-alltrails-growing-your-app-to-1m-paid-subscribers)). That is five years after launch. The lesson for Ancient Trees is not to wait five years but to notice that the import shape only started producing content once the app captured something the user was doing anyway. The tick-off with a GPS fix and a camera prompt is that something.

## The first thousand came from one gathering place, usually one the founder already belonged to

Every documented first-thousand story in the notes runs through a single pre-existing place where the right people already were. **Wikiloc announced itself in one post to a Spanish GPS and hiking forum on its launch day in April 2006, and by the next morning about 150 people had registered and shared about 200 routes**, some from Patagonia and Japan ([Wikiloc about us](https://www.wikiloc.com/wikiloc/about-us.do); [El Español](https://www.elespanol.com/omicrono/tecnologia/20210605/wikiloc-espanola-triunfa-rutas-imposibles-empezo-hobby/586442340_0.html)). A 2014 profile gives 140 registrations and 50 routes instead, so the exact figures are uncertain, but the shape is not: the forum members already held GPS tracks on their devices and had no map to put them on ([Roge Blasco](https://rogeblasco.blog/2014/01/19/jordi-lopez-ramot-wikiloc-la-enciclopedia-del-excursionismo/)). Geocaching's seed was the sci.geo.satellite-nav Usenet group and a mailing list that already existed before the site; the previous hub owner handed leadership to the new site in September 2000, Slashdot hit on 25 September and took down the bedroom server, the New York Times followed in October and CNN in December, and by year end Oregon and Washington each had about 300 caches ([Geocaching.com history](https://www.geocaching.com/about/history.aspx); [Oregon Encyclopedia](https://www.oregonencyclopedia.org/articles/geocaching/)). Atlas Obscura's gathering place was Boing Boing's readership, which gave about 200,000 unique visitors a month in year one "without paid promotion" ([Get Together](https://gettogether.substack.com/p/the-national-geographic-of-the-digital)). Untappd's founders met on Twitter, ran in Foursquare circles, and credit one Mashable article on 5 November 2010, two months after the private beta, as the tipping point; "not a PR campaign", one journalist's enthusiasm ([Braze](https://www.braze.com/resources/articles/engage-millions-users-mobile-community-building-lessons-untappd)).

Where no community existed, founders hand-picked and equipped the first users. Strava's founders are reported to have sent ten Garmin head units to passionate cyclists on the two coasts in 2008 and run East-versus-West competitions during that year's Tour de France; a second secondary source says 300 units, and no primary source settles it ([Techstadium](https://techstadium.substack.com/p/strava-analyzing-the-growth-playbook); [Mind Your Surroundings](https://mindyoursurroundings.beehiiv.com/p/strava)). What is consistent is the posture, in co-founder Gainey's words: "go an inch wide with this one group, we're gonna go mile deep with them" ([Alexander Jarvis](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/strava-doing-things-that-dont-scale)). Lenny's data has Strava at about 100 direct friends and about 1,000 users after twelve months by word of mouth, and the company at roughly 20,000 users when it raised its first institutional round in January 2011 ([Lenny's Newsletter](https://lennysnewsletter.com/i/38506758/calm); [Wikipedia: Strava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strava)). Polarsteps, the nearest case to Ancient Trees in geography and temperament (four Amsterdam founders with day jobs, no marketing spend ever), had "a few hundred" users after its 2015 innovation-award final, which the founders entered purely for visibility, and 20,000 a year after launch; the founders attribute growth to users recruiting users and four- and five-star App Store ratings, and no source names a press, blogger or forum channel for 2015 ([Emerce 2018](https://www.emerce.nl/interviews/polarsteps-reis-plannen-tracken-n-bewaren); [Emerce 2016](https://www.emerce.nl/?p=680408)).

The broader data agree. Across Lenny Rachitsky's 100+ consumer companies, **going where the target audience already hangs out was the most common tactic at over 50 percent and the one he calls highest ROI**; targeted outreach to strangers came second at about 30 percent and was mostly marketplaces recruiting supply; press featured in over 20 percent; friends and colleagues in about 20 percent; viral content and physical placement were rare at about 10 percent each. Most companies got there on one or two channels and none on more than three ([Lenny's Newsletter](https://lennysnewsletter.com/i/38506758/calm); [Indie Hackers reprint](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/how-the-biggest-consumer-apps-got-their-first-1-000-users-60945ab70a)). The examples that fit a maker with no budget are the small ones: Hipcamp's first roughly 100 users from a single table at REI, Discord's from one Final Fantasy subreddit, Etsy's from one craft forum. The hit rate of cold outreach depends on whether the ask flatters work the person already does: Behance asked its 100 most admired designers and nearly all agreed, Patreon asked 40 creators and all declined ([Lenny's Newsletter](https://lennysnewsletter.com/i/38506758/calm)). Paul Graham's essay underneath all this says to recruit users manually, start in a deliberately narrow market, and expect that big launches and partnerships with large organisations "usually don't work" ([Paul Graham](https://paulgraham.com/ds.html)).

One pattern deserves separate mention because it is the only one in the notes where a third party's distribution turned into contributors rather than readers. Wikiloc's two largest jumps were gifts from Google: a 2006 Maps mashup contest win that raised registrations 2,000 percent in three months (one source dates it 2008), and a Google Earth layer from August 2008 that the founder calls "enormous" growth. He "fought hard" so routes in that layer kept a link to the author's profile instead of being anonymised ([El Español](https://www.elespanol.com/omicrono/tecnologia/20210605/wikiloc-espanola-triunfa-rutas-imposibles-empezo-hobby/586442340_0.html); [Xataka](https://www.xataka.com/empresas-y-economia/exito-silencioso-llamado-wikiloc-12-millones-usuarios-referencia-mundial-made-in-spain)). Attribution was the mechanism that converted a platform's traffic into a contributor's reason to keep uploading. AllTrails' equivalent was the January 2012 redirect of National Geographic's dying Topo.com, about 300,000 registered users, into AllTrails' 200,000 ([TechCrunch 2012](https://techcrunch.com/2012/01/17/alltrails-partners-with-national-geographic-launches-redesigned-co-branded-website)). Neither can be planned; both were caught because the product was ready and the founders said yes.

## Contribution became self-sustaining when uploading cost nothing and somebody answered

The notes are consistent about what turned a visitor into a contributor, and it was not gamification. In the three catalogues the converter was a human on the other side. On iNaturalist users confirm or improve each other's identifications and "can't just mark identifications as incorrect"; an observation earns Research Grade once the community agrees and then flows to GBIF, which nearly half of the first million did ([Berkeley I School](https://www.ischool.berkeley.edu/news/2022/new-york-times-profiles-app-inaturalist-created-mims-alumni); [California Academy of Sciences](https://www.calacademy.org/press/releases/citizen-science-network-inaturalist-celebrates-millionth-nature-observation-as-it)). On Geocaching the game loop is the contribution: hiding a cache is how you play, every cache has a logbook finders sign, and the site sorted caches by distance from the user from day one ([Geocaching HQ transcript](https://www.geocaching.com/blog/?p=66865); [Oregon Encyclopedia](https://www.oregonencyclopedia.org/articles/geocaching/)). At Atlas Obscura a submission was answered by an editor who published it, and "a handful of users visited almost daily to create and edit entries" in year one; ten years on the site was still "fueled by a community of super contributors" ([Get Together](https://gettogether.substack.com/p/the-national-geographic-of-the-digital)). **Atlas Obscura is also the clearest case of a tactic being retired: the "anyone can edit anyone's entry" wiki of June 2009 became submit-to-volunteer-editors by March 2010 and a staffed editorial model by 2012** ([Boing Boing](https://boingboing.net/2009/06/15/introducing-the-atla.html); [Urban Omnibus 2012](https://urbanomnibus.net/2012/03/atlas-obscura/)).

In the log apps the converter was that the upload was useful to the uploader alone and comparison made a stranger's log valuable to you. Strava's leaderboard meant beta cyclists were "calling in sick to work so they could go ride their bikes"; the 2011 phone app, which removed the GPS-plus-laptop step, "sharply increased engagement" ([Contrary Research](https://research.contrary.com/company/strava); [Techstadium](https://techstadium.substack.com/p/strava-analyzing-the-growth-playbook)). Waze's first hook was a speed-camera add-on; users mapped every camera in Israel within three days because each one served the driver who added it ([Stanford transcript](https://stvp.stanford.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2024/09/early-days-of-waze-transcript.pdf)). Polarsteps' sharing audience is by definition non-users, the family at home, so every trip recruits followers; the printed Travel Book that became 100 percent of revenue was requested by users, not planned ([Peecho case study](https://www.peecho.com/case-studies/polarsteps)). Untappd is the one app with badges from launch, copied from Foursquare, with users voting every two months on the next wave; its acquisition loop was breweries claiming their listing and putting up "check in here" signs, the venue as champion ([Braze](https://www.braze.com/resources/articles/engage-millions-users-mobile-community-building-lessons-untappd)).

Ambassadors, chapters and events are a second-phase pattern in this record. Yelp's Elite Squad, from 2005, was a recognition programme for the most prolific existing reviewers, used to open each new city, and Notion's community programme started with 20 super fans the company found already running meetups and YouTube channels ([Chief Executive](https://chiefexecutive.net/?p=41982); [Lima blog summary](https://limablog.substack.com/p/building-a-community-that-fuels-growth)). The one early-years event with a documented effect is Obscura Day: nine months after launch, 20 March 2010, about 4,000 people at 80 events in 20 countries, promoted by National Geographic, and the case study's thesis is that the site "transformed from a mere wiki into a community because of" it ([Get Together](https://gettogether.substack.com/p/the-national-geographic-of-the-digital); [Make:](https://makezine.com/article/home/obscura-day-march-20-2010/)). iNaturalist's growth engine was attaching to organised counting events (the 2011 Global Amphibian and Reptile BioBlitzes) and institutions that already had naturalists, and its "roughly doubling each year" dates from 2012, the year after those partnerships and a co-founder joining ([Wikipedia: iNaturalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INaturalist); [iNaturalist About](https://www.inaturalist.org/pages/about.html)). Geocaching's first Event Cache came in March 2001, six months after launch ([Geocaching milestones](https://www.geocaching.com/blog/?p=147963)). No source in any of the four notes documents a badge or points system as the early converter at AllTrails, komoot, Wikiloc, Strava, Polarsteps, iNaturalist, Geocaching or Atlas Obscura, and nothing dates komoot's Pioneers programme before 2015. Gamification may simply be undocumented, but the burden is on it.

## Timing ran in years, and Google recoveries run in core-update cycles

The honest timeline for every bootstrapped case is slow. iNaturalist ran three years as a side project before an LLC and six before an institutional home ([iNaturalist About](https://www.inaturalist.org/pages/about.html)). Strava was bootstrapped for about three years to 20,000 users ([Wikipedia: Strava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strava)). Wikiloc's founder kept a day job for two years and left it in 2008 ([Maillot Mag](https://www.maillotmag.com/afondo/entrevista-jordi-ramot-ceo-y-fundador-de-wikiloc)). Geocaching's third founder only went full-time in late 2005, five years in, and British Columbia took eighteen months to reach 300 caches ([Geocaching.com history](https://www.geocaching.com/about/history.aspx); [GC1V4YH](https://www.geocaching.com/geocache/GC1V4YH)). Pinterest had fewer than 10,000 users in its first nine months and its founder personally emailed the first 5,000 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=532060)). Polarsteps' "keerpunt" was the March 2016 round that let the founders quit their jobs, a year after launch ([Emerce 2018](https://www.emerce.nl/interviews/polarsteps-reis-plannen-tracken-n-bewaren)). The only quick first-thousands (Wikiloc overnight, Geocaching in four months, Untappd in ten weeks) happened where a hungry community with the right gear already existed and the product caught it. The notes also flag a retention warning from a community travel app: good early content does not bring people back if they travel rarely ([HN comment](https://hnrss.org/user?id=alex_c)). Ancient Trees' own 2026-10-23 answer that return is monthly, like Polarsteps, is consistent with that and should set expectations for what "active phones" can look like.

The Google situation adds a second clock. Of 360+ sites Glenn Gabe tracked after the September 2023 helpful-content update, none had recovered by March 2024; the first group of 30 to 47 surged only with the August 2024 core update ([ppc.land](https://ppc.land/glenn-gabe/); [RankMath](https://rankmath.com/google-update/august-2024-core-update/)). A vendor-compiled dataset of about 400 sites puts meaningful recovery (20 percent or more of lost traffic) at 22 percent, full recovery at 12 to 24+ months, and finds that sites that pruned 40 to 60 percent of content before adding anything recovered at about three times the rate, with first-person experience language correlating positively ([The Stacc](https://thestacc.com/blog/helpful-content-update-recovery/), vendor, secondhand). The best-documented full recovery, HouseFresh, took two years and one month, and its editor says no single change did it; a YouTube channel and a collaboration that lifted brand searches were part of the path ([ppc.land on HouseFresh](https://ppc.land/housefresh-achieves-notable-traffic-recovery-after-google-algorithm-impacts-2/)). Every documented recovery date coincides with a core update, not with the day the site changed. The pruning and the noindex split are already done; the first-hand material the recoveries correlate with is readers' photographs and quoted words, which is also the phase 1 goal, so the two lanes are one lane. Nothing on-page within six weeks will move Search Console, and nothing numeric here is from a primary Search Console export.

App Store search is a real channel but a keyword-bound one. Apple says about 70 percent of store visitors use search and almost 65 percent of downloads follow a search; Sensor Tower independently put search at 59 percent of iOS downloads in 2020 ([Apple Ads Advanced](https://ads.apple.com/app-store/advanced); [Statista, Sensor Tower data](https://www-statista-com.ezproxy.canberra.edu.au/statistics/1227712/global-distribution-app-download-source-apple-app-store)). Featuring is the one documented large lever, a median lift of 685 percent for App of the Day over a week, and **29 percent of apps featured in the redesigned store had under 10,000 downloads at the time** ([Sensor Tower](https://sensortower.com/blog/ios-11-featuring-impact)). Polarsteps got App of the Day in 18 countries with zero marketing spend ([Emerce 2018](https://www.emerce.nl/interviews/polarsteps-reis-plannen-tracken-n-bewaren)). No source gives hard numbers on what a small unfeatured app earns from organic store search, and Lenny's data does not list app stores among first-1,000 tactics. Treat the store as the default install path for people who already search "old trees", keep the metadata honest and keyword-bearing, and treat featuring as a free nomination already filed.

## What fits Ancient Trees, tactic by tactic

The project's state, read against the nine cases, is unusual in one way: it has AllTrails' catalogue and Polarsteps' team. 3,800 trees in 230 cities is more seed than any hand-seeded case had after years, and the hand-seeded cases are the ones that grew contributors. Seven volunteer ambassadors and 35 accounts are roughly where Atlas Obscura's "handful" and Strava's ten cyclists were. The chicken-and-egg problem here is therefore not content; it is density. Chen's atomic network is the smallest group where enough people have enough of each other to stay, usually smaller and narrower than founders expect (Uber at one San Francisco station at one time), and the hard side is recruited first ([Talks at Google summary](https://notes.laratranslate.com/andrew-chen-the-cold-start-problem-how-to-start-and-scale-network-effects-talks-at-google-ef7d2bfd/summary); [Product #31](https://productandrew.substack.com/p/product-31)). For a tree map the atomic network is one city, or one walk, where a visitor reliably meets another person's photograph and a contributor sees their photo used; it is not "the Netherlands". The hard side is the person who photographs and submits, not the person who browses. Seven ambassadors spread over seven places means no place ever gets dense.

| Tactic | Evidence in the nine cases | Fit for Ancient Trees now |
|---|---|---|
| Seed content yourself | AllTrails 45k trails; AO, Geocaching, iNat first hundred by founders | Done. More seeding is the failure mode (Chen's "stuck on the tool"); supply is paused anyway |
| One gathering place, by hand | Wikiloc forum, Geocaching Usenet, AO on Boing Boing, Discord subreddit, Hipcamp REI table; Lenny over 50 percent | Strongest fit. The r/sicily fig is this in miniature. Two or three places, not more |
| Targeted strangers on the supply side | Behance 100 designers (nearly all yes), Strava ten cyclists, Lenny about 30 percent | Strong fit: people who already photograph old trees (Commons uploaders, tree-walk organisers). Ask must flatter existing work |
| Single-player reason to contribute | Strava analytics, Waze cameras, Wikiloc GPX viewer, Polarsteps dot | Strong fit: the tick-off, GPS fix, "your photo is the lead image". Reciprocity must be fast and visible |
| Recognition of existing contributors | Yelp Elite (2005), Notion's 20 found super fans | Already in place as the komoot-Pioneer badge; next step is visibility, not more invitations |
| Events | Obscura Day (9 months in, 27 cities), BioBlitz, Event Cache | Fits only if an ambassador hosts one walk; not a one-person job. Thin for this scale |
| Press | Untappd Mashable, Geocaching Slashdot/NYT, Udemy; Lenny over 20 percent | Lottery ticket, already bought (Lisbon article, Show HN). Do not plan around it |
| Platform gift | Wikiloc Google Earth layer, AllTrails Topo.com redirect, Polarsteps App of the Day | Cannot be planned; keep attribution on every reader photo so any traffic converts to contributors |
| SEO | AllTrails "legacy SEO", komoot route pages | The engine that worked for the catalogue shape and is now demoted; recovery is cycle-bound, not week-bound |
| App Store search | Apple 65 percent of downloads; featuring +685 percent; no first-1,000 evidence | Keep-warm channel; honest ASO; nomination filed |
| Badges and gamification | Untappd only; absent from the other eight early records | No evidence it converts early; park |
| Big partnerships | Graham: "practically nothing"; AllTrails NatGeo was an exception at 200k users | Not now |
| Daily social posting | Not in any first-1,000 record in the notes | Not supported by evidence, and the owner does not want it |

Two constraints from the project's own rules shape the fit. Ambassadors are not to be asked for links or badges on their sites and a photo-only reader is invited only after two photographs in a place, so the ambassador lane is recognition and a single possible event, never a recruitment drive. And a stranger's photograph with a GPS fix goes live at once and their words are quoted on the page, which is already the fast reciprocity the catalogues depended on; the gap is that few strangers know it will happen before they send.

## Recommendation: the next six weeks

Ranked. Each item names the case it leans on and how thin the evidence is.

**1. Choose one atomic place and make it dense before anything else.** Pick one city on the kept set (a page Google still shows) where an ambassador or Hidde already lives, with enough trees inside two kilometres for one walk. Work it until every tree on that walk has an approved photograph and at least two carry a reader's photo and quoted words. Measure it as "a visitor there meets another person's photo", not as trees added. Evidence: Chen's atomic network, Strava's "inch wide, mile deep", Hipcamp's one table. Thin on the exact size needed; no source gives a density threshold for a place-based map.

**2. Write to the people who already photograph old trees in that place, one by one.** Commons uploaders of that city's tree photographs, the organiser of its tree walk, the person who posted about a tree in its subreddit or Facebook group. The ask names one specific tree and flatters work they already did (the Behance pattern), never asks them to start a habit (the Patreon pattern). Drafts are machine-written, Hidde sends, perhaps ten in six weeks. Evidence: Lenny's targeted-strangers tactic, Behance's near-total yes rate. Thin on yield for this kind of ask; no documented hit rate for hobby photographers.

**3. Go where tree lovers already gather, in two places at most, and answer rather than post.** The r/sicily fig is the model: a thread names a tree, the tree goes up, the thread hears "it's on the map now". Pick the two forums or subreddits with the most old-tree threads in the atomic place's language and watch them; reply when a tree is named. This is a few replies a week, not daily posting. Evidence: Lenny's most common and highest-ROI tactic, Wikiloc's one forum post, Discord's one subreddit; nobody succeeded on more than three channels. Strong.

**4. Make the single-player loop visible at first run and close it fast.** The honest substitute for fake seeding is onboarding that asks for one photograph of a tree near you; the reward the catalogues depended on is a human answer within hours (published, quoted, the "it's live" mail). Check that a new app user learns before their first photo that it becomes the lead image on the page and that their words are quoted. Evidence: Strava and Waze single-player hooks, iNaturalist and Atlas Obscura reciprocity. Medium; the onboarding-seeding claim rests on one vendor post.

**5. Keep the lottery tickets, buy no more.** The featuring nomination is filed, Show HN posted, the Lisbon article out. Keep App Store metadata keyword-bearing. Do not file more press or spend a window on it. Evidence: press at over 20 percent in Lenny's data but never planned in any of the nine cases. Strong on the "do not plan around it".

**6. Offer, do not organise, one walk.** If an ambassador in the atomic place wants to host a tree walk, support it with a printed list and the page link; otherwise nothing. Evidence: Obscura Day made Atlas Obscura a community, but with 27 host cities and National Geographic behind it. Thin fit for one person; park unless volunteered.

**What not to do in these six weeks.** Do not open new cities or spread the ambassadors across more places; density beats breadth in every case read. Do not add badges, points or streaks; no early-years record except Untappd's supports them as a converter, and Untappd copied Foursquare in a category where check-ins were already the habit. Do not pursue partnerships with large organisations; Graham's and the notes' record is "practically nothing" unless the partner is dying and hands over its users. Do not expect on-page work to move Google within the period; measure the demotion in core-update cycles, and treat reader photographs and quoted words as the recovery material. Do not use more than three channels; do not post daily; do not fake contributors or seed under other names, which the platforms' own terms and the HN consensus both reject ([adigaskell.org](https://adigaskell.org/?p=1751)).

**Where the evidence is thin, in one place.** Strava's first-user numbers conflict (ten versus 300 Garmin units) and come from secondary posts. Untappd's "100,000 users at one year" is a designer's portfolio, not the company. komoot's early record is nearly empty; its first-users tactic is inferred (App Store, near-empty category) and not sourced. Polarsteps' 2015 channel is not named by any source. iNaturalist's first non-staff users in 2008 to 2010 are undocumented because the founder's own retrospective sat behind a bot wall. No ambassador programme has a published yield per ambassador at this scale. The Google recovery numbers are vendor-compiled and secondhand. And no case anywhere in the four notes is a solo maker running a place-based photo app from hundreds to thousands of contributors; the nearest are Hipcamp's first 100 from one table, Wikiloc's one forum, and Polarsteps' first year, and all three had either a hungry pre-existing community or more than one founder.

## Conclusion

The question was how content apps solved chicken-and-egg, and the answer across nine cases is that they mostly did not solve it as a two-sided problem at all. Either the content was there before the users (import), or the content was the user's own log and arrived with them, or a founder wrote the first hundred items and a single pre-existing crowd filled in the rest. Ancient Trees has done the first of these at a scale none of the hand-seeded cases reached, which is why the project reads as "stuck on the tool": the import shape produces readers, and in AllTrails' case it took five years and a recording feature before readers became contributors. The move the record supports is to stop behaving like the import shape and start behaving like the hand-seeded one, which means one place, ten named people, two forums, and a reciprocity loop that closes within hours.

The second implication is about clocks. The project is currently measured on Google's clock and the App Store's, both of which are outside its control and run in months. The cases that grew contributors were measured on a different clock, density in one place, which a single person can move in six weeks and which also happens to produce the first-hand material the Google recoveries correlate with. Switching the headline measure for this phase from impressions to "how many of this one city's trees carry a stranger's photograph" would make the six weeks legible, and would be the first time the project's own numbers matched the shape of the evidence.
