# How AllTrails, Wikiloc and komoot solved the chicken-and-egg problem (2006 to 2015)

Research notes, 2026-10-23. Scope: the first years of three trail apps. Everything dated is cited; "inferred" means my reading, not a source. The three companies solved the cold start in three different ways: AllTrails imported public datasets and then bought a dying competitor's users; Wikiloc seeded with the founder's own rides and one forum post, then rode a Google distribution deal; komoot had no user content problem at all at first, because its first product was an algorithm over OpenStreetMap, and community content came later.

## Key question 1: Did they seed content themselves before asking users? Which datasets?

### Takeaway
Two of three seeded. AllTrails launched in December 2010 with a database built from public US government data (US Forest Service, National Park Service) plus crowd edits checked by an editorial team, and had 45,000 North American trails by November 2011. komoot never needed seeded trails: its product was route planning computed from OpenStreetMap, so the "content" existed on day one. Wikiloc seeded with the founder's own weekend bike routes, built for a group of friends, and then got about 200 routes in one night from a forum announcement.

### Cited Findings

**AllTrails**
- Founded 2010 by Russell Cook, incubated in AngelPad's inaugural class; official launch December 2010. — [Wikipedia: AllTrails](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AllTrails)
- Cook's own account of the motivation: personal frustration after exhausting Pacific Northwest hikes ("why can't I find these hidden gems easily?") and a bet that phone GPS would get very good within five years ("Everyone was going to have a Garmin in their pocket"). — [Inc.](https://www.inc.com/chris-gayomali-2/how-alltrails-got-everyone-to-go-outside/91022169) (snippet from search; the page itself returned 403 on fetch)
- In 2011, "a few short months after AllTrails' launch", NASA released its ASTER L2 elevation dataset publicly, which Cook called "lucky timing" and which the article says proved crucial to the mapping. — [Inc.](https://www.inc.com/chris-gayomali-2/how-alltrails-got-everyone-to-go-outside/91022169) (snippet only)
- November 2011: "The company has catalogued 45K outdoor trails in North America"; users review and browse trails by location, popularity, recency, length and "dog friendliness"; six people in the former Squarespace offices in SOMA; tagline "Yelp for the outdoors"; seed round of $400K from 500 Startups and 2020 Ventures; an Android app had just been added to the iPhone app. The article does not say how the 45K trails were gathered. — [TechCrunch, 2011-11-13](https://techcrunch.com/2011/11/13/alltrails-pockets-400k-from-500-startups-and-more/)
- January 2012: AllTrails "pulls in publicly available data, such as from the U.S. Forest Service and National Park Service, and combines it with crowd-sourced efforts." Cook: "The information is out there, it's all over the place, and you can't really trust it." "Any user can submit edits to new trails, then we have a team of editors that make sure it's accurate" before publishing; editors "sometimes call the park to check accuracy." Cook: "We go back through it with a team of editors, and a lot of our top users help as well." The database was built for North America first. — [TechCrunch, 2012-01-17](https://techcrunch.com/2012/01/17/alltrails-partners-with-national-geographic-launches-redesigned-co-branded-website)
- A secondary source claims the company "launched with ~35,000 trails"; not corroborated elsewhere. — [search snippet, uncorroborated](https://businessmodelcanvastemplate.com/blogs/brief-history/alltrails-brief-history)
- AllTrails is today a member of the OpenStreetMap US Trails Working Group with NPS, USFS and USGS (a later fact, included only to show the dataset lineage persists). — [USGS](https://www.usgs.gov/national-digital-trails/through-its-trails-stewardship-initiative-openstreetmap-us-leading-efforts)

**Wikiloc**
- Started as a personal project in Girona, 2006: Ramot (a computer scientist who had worked on public-sector GIS: cadastre, urban planning, map digitisation, then fleet tracking) posted his own weekend mountain-bike routes with tips and photos. — [Xataka](https://www.xataka.com/empresas-y-economia/exito-silencioso-llamado-wikiloc-12-millones-usuarios-referencia-mundial-made-in-spain); [Maillot Mag interview](https://www.maillotmag.com/afondo/entrevista-jordi-ramot-ceo-y-fundador-de-wikiloc)
- Ramot's own words: it began as "una página web para compartir rutas con mis amigos"; before that he emailed friends a weekly report of his weekend hikes; friends said "Yo también quiero" and wanted profiles of their own. — [El Español, 2021-06-05](https://www.elespanol.com/omicrono/tecnologia/20210605/wikiloc-espanola-triunfa-rutas-imposibles-empezo-hobby/586442340_0.html)
- The core tool was a program that took a Garmin GPX file and drew it on a map automatically; earlier prototypes overlaid routes on an IGN topographic map ("cientos de horas de trabajo"). He first gave login profiles and route albums to his riding group, then "decidí abrirlo a todo el mundo". He says he borrowed ideas from Panoramio. First name was "Trailomatics", rejected by colleague Montse. — [Maillot Mag](https://www.maillotmag.com/afondo/entrevista-jordi-ramot-ceo-y-fundador-de-wikiloc)
- The 2014 Blasco profile adds that he had been sending hiking photos to friends while working in Vancouver, and launched on his birthday, 6 April 2006. (Wikiloc's own page says 7 April; Wikipedia cites a blog post "Wikiloc is online" of 28 June 2006.) — [Roge Blasco, 2014-01-19](https://rogeblasco.blog/2014/01/19/jordi-lopez-ramot-wikiloc-la-enciclopedia-del-excursionismo/); [Wikiloc about us](https://www.wikiloc.com/wikiloc/about-us.do); [Wikipedia: Wikiloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikiloc)
- No imported dataset at all: the first-night content came from forum members' own GPS tracks (see question 2). — [Wikiloc about us](https://www.wikiloc.com/wikiloc/about-us.do)
- Stack listed by Wikipedia: Linux, PostgreSQL/PostGIS, Apache, Hibernate, GEOS, GDAL, PROJ.4, Python. — [Wikipedia: Wikiloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikiloc)

**komoot**
- Founded 22 February 2010 in Potsdam by six friends from Austria and Germany: Jonas Spengler, Markus Hallermann, Tobias Hallermann, Christoph Lingg, Daniel Gard, Jan Heuer; idea in 2009; first app in the Apple App Store in 2010; early money from the Brandenburg early-stage fund (ILB) "a few months after the start". — [komoot.business story page](https://komoot.business/en/about/story-mission); [ILB founder profile](https://www.ilb.de/de/existenzgruendung/existenzgruendung-gruenderprofile/komoot-gmbh/); [WirtschaftsWoche, 2025](https://www.wiwo.de/technologie/digitale-welt/komoot-wander-app-wird-verkauft/100115256.html)
- Hallermann's origin story: planning an Alpine crossing at 16 took weeks of books and counting contour lines; "Das muss doch einfacher gehen!" Mission: outdoor adventures for people without map-and-compass skills. — [TN-Deutschland interview](https://www.tn-deutschland.com/das-muss-doch-einfacher-gehen/)
- Maps "mostly based on OpenStreetMap"; the core technology is Leaflet, GraphHopper routing and OSM data, all open source. — [de.wikipedia: Komoot](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Komoot); [bikepacking.com](https://bikepacking.com/plog/when-we-get-komooted/)
- Hallermann on the early market (OMR podcast, paraphrased by OMR): they found "sehr viel grüne Wiese" (open field) and "viel Technologie selbst entwickeln müssen", including their own subscription billing. Routes are generated "aus der User-Community heraus" (OMR's wording). — [OMR Daily](https://omr.com/de/daily/omr-podcast-komoot)
- komoot's 2024 release describes the engine: routing combines "data from millions of recorded tours and OpenStreetMap maps with local community input such as photos, tips and highlights." — [komoot newsroom, 2024](https://newsroom.komoot.com/238612-komoot-breaks-the-40-million-mark-for-registered-users)
- A critical bikepacking.com essay says the founders conceived it as a way to algorithmically find trails from a hotel on a business trip (secondhand), and argues user data went into komoot rather than back to OSM. — [bikepacking.com](https://bikepacking.com/plog/when-we-get-komooted/)

### Inferences
- AllTrails' real seed was free US federal GIS (USFS, NPS) plus NASA elevation; "crowd-sourced" in 2011 to 2012 meant edits to that base checked by staff, not user-recorded tracks. The user layer (reviews, photos, recordings) came on top of an already-complete catalogue.
- komoot sidestepped the problem by making the map compute the content: OSM plus a router gives a useful route in any region with no community. User content (Highlights, photos) is additive, not load-bearing, in the first years.
- Wikiloc is the only one of the three that was genuinely community-first from night one, and it worked because the founder went to a place where people already held the content (GPS tracks on their devices) and had no way to show it on a map.

### Gaps
- No source states exactly which datasets AllTrails loaded at launch (December 2010) or how many trails it had on day one; the 35,000 figure is from an unreliable blog.
- Nothing found on whether komoot imported any trail or POI dataset beyond OSM in 2010 to 2012, nor on how the first Highlights were seeded (staff, founders or users).
- Inc. magazine's full Cook interview (likely richest primary source on AllTrails' origins) could not be fetched (403); only search snippets used.

## Key question 2: How did the first thousand users arrive?

### Takeaway
Wikiloc: one forum post to a GPS/hiking forum on launch day gave about 150 registrations and about 200 routes by the next morning, then a Google Maps API contest win in 2006 produced a 2,000 percent registration jump in three months, and a Google Earth layer in 2008 gave "enormous" growth. AllTrails: SEO on trail pages ("legacy SEO" is the phrase its later CEO uses), an accelerator, a seed round, early app-store presence, and in January 2012 a partnership that redirected National Geographic's dying Topo.com (around 300,000 registered users) into AllTrails (200,000 users). komoot: the record is thin; the documented levers are the App Store (2010), a state fund, and algorithmic route pages that dominated search; no source names a first-users tactic.

## Key question 3: What was the single-player value before the community existed?

### Takeaway
AllTrails: a searchable, filterable catalogue of government trail data on a map with an editorial check ("you can't really trust" the scattered sources). komoot: a route the algorithm computes for you, with elevation and surface, in a region you pay once for; no other user needed. Wikiloc: a free place to put your own GPX file and see it on a map (Google Maps did not exist when Ramot started prototyping), and a profile with a route album.

### Cited Findings
- Wikiloc: the founding problem was that friends emailed photos with no map; Ramot built a tool to upload a Garmin GPX and see it on a map automatically, with per-person profiles and route albums. — [Maillot Mag](https://www.maillotmag.com/afondo/entrevista-jordi-ramot-ceo-y-fundador-de-wikiloc)
- Wikiloc: GPX downloads are free; registration optional but needed to download; paid only for sending to a Garmin or phone. — [Wikipedia: Wikiloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikiloc)
- AllTrails 2011: browse by location, popularity, recency, length, dog-friendliness; 2012: editors verify imported public data, sometimes by phoning the park. — [TechCrunch 2011](https://techcrunch.com/2011/11/13/alltrails-pockets-400k-from-500-startups-and-more/); [TechCrunch 2012](https://techcrunch.com/2012/01/17/alltrails-partners-with-national-geographic-launches-redesigned-co-branded-website)
- AllTrails 2012: premium (AllTrails Pro) sold National Geographic TOPO! and Trails Illustrated maps, GPS routes online and on mobile, retail discounts. — [TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/alltrails-the-yelp-for-outdoor-pursuits-partners-with-national-geographic-maps); [PR Newswire 2012](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/alltrails-the-leading-digital-network-for-outdoor-enthusiasts-celebrates-one-million-mobile-installs-and-announces-the-retail-launch-of-the-ultimate-outdoor-mapkit-177633061.html)
- komoot: routes planned on PC or phone with elevation profile, duration, GPX export, voice navigation; the first region free, further regions paid once. — [de.wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Komoot)
- komoot: "one of the first route-planning apps on the market"; early revenue from selling single maps. — [OMR Daily](https://omr.com/de/daily/omr-podcast-komoot)

### Inferences
- In all three, the single-player product was a map tool, not a community: a catalogue (AllTrails), a planner (komoot), a GPX viewer and personal album (Wikiloc). Reviews, Highlights and photos arrived as a second layer.

## Key question 4: When did contributions become self-sustaining, and what triggered it?

### Takeaway
Wikiloc was self-sustaining from the first night: GPS owners already had tracks and wanted a map to put them on; attribution (author link on every route, fought for in the Google Earth deal) kept them contributing. AllTrails' contributions were editorial until at least 2012 (edits submitted by users, approved by staff and "top users"); the ratings/reviews/photos/recordings flywheel is described by Schneidermann as the fix for bad data after 2015, when native apps and recording made it "passive and active feedback loops". komoot's Highlights and photo features are documented as live by September 2014, but no source dates their launch or a tipping point.

### Cited Findings
- Wikiloc: contributors are not paid; content is proprietary; Ramot's principle "the user goes first" (blog, December 2010). — [Wikipedia: Wikiloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikiloc); [Wikiloc blog](http://wikiloc.blogspot.com/)
- Wikiloc, 2008 Google Earth layer: Ramot fought to keep author attribution with a profile link rather than anonymised routes. — [Xataka](https://www.xataka.com/empresas-y-economia/exito-silencioso-llamado-wikiloc-12-millones-usuarios-referencia-mundial-made-in-spain)
- Wikiloc feature cadence from the blog: May 2010 activity filters; September 2010 photo bar (350,000+ photos and videos); February 2012 private messaging between users and new activities; June 2012 Android search packs (100 downloads per pack). — [Wikiloc blog](http://wikiloc.blogspot.com/)
- Wikiloc numbers: 1.8M routes (Jan 2014), 2M "excursiones" (2014), 3.7M users and 8.8M routes in 190 countries (June 2018), 8M members and 24.5M routes (June 2021), 12M users (Xataka, 2021-22), 20M members and 75M routes (2026). — [Roge Blasco](https://rogeblasco.blog/2014/01/19/jordi-lopez-ramot-wikiloc-la-enciclopedia-del-excursionismo/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikiloc); [IDEE blog 2018](https://blog-idee.blogspot.com/2018/06/conferencia-de-jordi-ramot-sobre-wikiloc.html); [El Español](https://www.elespanol.com/omicrono/tecnologia/20210605/wikiloc-espanola-triunfa-rutas-imposibles-empezo-hobby/586442340_0.html); [Todomountainbike 2026](https://www.todomountainbike.net/equipamiento/wikiloc-celebra-20-anos-convertida-referencia-mundial-descubrir-rutas-al-aire-libre)
- AllTrails 2012: user edits go through an editor team, helped by "a lot of our top users". — [TechCrunch 2012](https://techcrunch.com/2012/01/17/alltrails-partners-with-national-geographic-launches-redesigned-co-branded-website)
- AllTrails 2015 onward (Schneidermann): "the product sucked"; trail data "how bad the data was" was the biggest problem, "all hands on deck" until fixed; users contribute ratings, reviews, photos and recordings; "We can't do it alone" because trails change (flooding, fires, fallen trees); native apps launched "around 2015" and became the main channel; the free tier drives the contribution flywheel so the paywall cannot be squeezed. — [Sub Club](https://subclub.com/episode/ron-schneidermann-alltrails-growing-your-app-to-1m-paid-subscribers)
- Schneidermann elsewhere: "passive and active feedback loops that we've been able to create where our users help curate our content"; he joined 2015 as COO and CMO into a team of six, focused on trail content quality and encouraging UGC. — [Branch podcast page](https://www.branch.io/resources/podcast/ceo-alltrails-ron-schneidermann-leading-a-small-team-to-enormous-growth/); [Startup Signals](https://startupsignals.substack.com/p/alltrails-getting-people-outdoors)
- komoot by September 2014: users "mark and share favorite spots on a tour route", follow others, share photos, inspiration feed of trail images. — [PR Newswire 2014](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/komoot-launches-in-the-us-to-inspire-your-outdoor-experiences-on-heels-of-becoming-europes-leading-inspiration-app-277079101.html)

### Inferences
- For AllTrails the trigger was recording: once the phone app recorded hikes (around 2015), every walk became a data contribution without a prompt; reviews and photos rode on that. Before that, contribution was a Wikipedia-style edit flow gated by staff.
- For komoot the loop is structural: a Highlight improves the next person's computed route, so the product rewards contribution without gamification; no evidence of ambassadors (the Pioneers programme) before 2015 was found.
- Wikiloc never needed a trigger because the upload was the product; the risk it managed was attribution loss, not low contribution.

## Key question 5: What did the founders say did NOT work?

### Takeaway
Thin on the record. AllTrails (Schneidermann, about 2015): the product and data were bad, a media play and programmatic ads were a stopgap "just trying to buy time", the hardcore backcountry segment would not pay, and $50/year was too high. komoot (Hallermann): the first subscription attempt failed and they had to build billing themselves. Wikiloc (Ramot): only the name Trailomatics, and AdSense being "less relevant" than direct sponsors.

### Cited Findings
- AllTrails: "the product sucked, it sucked"; early media and programmatic-ad experiments were "just trying to buy time"; backcountry users had "esoteric and extreme product requirements" and were unlikely to pay, so focus moved to casual users; $50/year Pro drew "50 bucks, like it's way too much"; tests at $30 and $15 were net-flat, $30 chosen. — [Sub Club](https://subclub.com/episode/ron-schneidermann-alltrails-growing-your-app-to-1m-paid-subscribers)
- AllTrails: Schneidermann took the company "from 6 people to 100 people, and through a really tough transition period" (no detail). — [Port of Entry Partners podcast](https://www.portofentrypartners.com/podcast/episode-6-ron-schneidermann-ceo-of-alltrails)
- komoot: the podcast teaser says introducing a subscription model initially failed; they had to develop much technology themselves, including subscription billing. — [OMR Daily](https://omr.com/de/daily/omr-podcast-komoot)
- Wikiloc: first name "Trailomatics" rejected; December 2010 blog post says AdSense was "less relevant" and opened direct sponsorship. — [Maillot Mag](https://www.maillotmag.com/afondo/entrevista-jordi-ramot-ceo-y-fundador-de-wikiloc); [Wikiloc blog](http://wikiloc.blogspot.com/)
- Wikiloc never took the founding as a business: "El proyecto nace sin ningún ánimo de lucro ni expectativas de ingresar nada de dinero"; server bills forced AdSense; white-label for parks and tourism offices stayed a minority; Ramot juggled a day job for two years and left it in 2008 ("en 2008 decidí dejarlo"); profitable "desde hacía mucho" before the Miura investment. — [Maillot Mag](https://www.maillotmag.com/afondo/entrevista-jordi-ramot-ceo-y-fundador-de-wikiloc); [El Español](https://www.elespanol.com/omicrono/tecnologia/20210605/wikiloc-espanola-triunfa-rutas-imposibles-empezo-hobby/586442340_0.html); [Xataka](https://www.xataka.com/empresas-y-economia/exito-silencioso-llamado-wikiloc-12-millones-usuarios-referencia-mundial-made-in-spain)

### Inferences
- The common failure named across two of the three is pricing and the first monetisation attempt, not acquisition; none of the founders describes a failed user-acquisition channel in the early years, which fits a story where organic search and a platform gift (Google Earth, Topo.com redirect, App Store) did the work.
