# How Strava, Untappd and Polarsteps got their first contributors and first thousand users (2008 to 2017)

Scope note: early years only, dated where the source dates it. "Inference" is marked. Several primary pages (Avola's Medium post, the CNBC 2016 piece, the Wine&More ten-year piece, the Leighton Hubbell badge portfolio) returned 403 to the fetch tool; what I report from them comes from search-result snippets and is flagged as such. I found no founder interview from any of the three companies that gives a day-by-day account of the first thousand users; the best material is per-app below.

## Key question 1: What was the single-player value on day one, and how did a personal log become shared content?

### Takeaway
All three started as a tool that was useful alone (a ride analysed, a beer remembered, a dot on a map for family at home), and the "shared" layer was already in the first build in the form of comparison or a feed, not bolted on later. Strava's was the most deliberately social from day one (a leaderboard before anything else); Polarsteps' sharing was the founding use case (friends and family following a sailor); Untappd copied Foursquare's check-in plus badges wholesale.

### Cited Findings
Strava
- The MVP (2008) was "initially just a leaderboard that tracked the efforts of a few athletes across a couple of Vermont and California cycling segments"; Horvath said cyclists' enthusiasm convinced them to invest by the end of 2008, and that beta athletes were "calling in sick to work so they could go ride their bikes" to attack the leaderboard. — [Contrary Research](https://research.contrary.com/company/strava)
- Strava initially operated as a website accepting uploads from Garmin devices; the first mobile app came in 2011 (Mark Gainey, Terra podcast, via Wikipedia). The original free tier allowed five uploads per month, further uploads needed payment. — [Wikipedia: Strava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strava)
- Segment leaderboards "grew out of cyclists' conversations about individual climbs". — [Wikipedia: Strava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strava)
- Horvath (VentureBeat, 2011): "What we're doing is trying to give an experience that's social fitness that focuses on comparing and competing." — [Techstadium, citing VentureBeat 2011](https://techstadium.substack.com/p/strava-analyzing-the-growth-playbook)
- Horvath (2022): "a subscription at the core of our business model since the very beginning"; growth is "one active person telling another". — [Fitt Insider podcast #137, May 2022](https://insider.fitt.co/137-michael-horvath-ceo-co-founder-of-strava/)

Untappd
- Summer 2010: Avola first talked with Mather about a social space for craft beer lovers; at the time Foursquare dominated check-ins and BeerAdvocate was the top beer-rating publication. September 2010: first prototype launched as a private beta, with no photo uploads, no ratings, no moderation. — [Untappd blog, "Cheers to 11 years"](https://untappd.com/blog/cheers-to-11-years-of-check-ins/1356)
- Avola was an early Foursquare user who wanted a beer-focused app on Foursquare's lines; he found Mather (designer) on Twitter; prototype built in under 24 hours, launched about two weeks later. — [Wine&More, "10 years of Untappd"](https://www.wineandmore.com/stories/10-years-of-untappd-beer-drinking/) (search snippet only; page returned 403)
- The early app was a location-aware mobile web app that used the Foursquare API for location. — [WapReview](https://WapReview.com/9999/) (search snippet)
- Badges were in from the start, borrowed from Foursquare's model. — [Miami New Times](https://www.miaminewtimes.com/?p=40411278) (search snippet)
- The three-part feed (Friends, Global, Nearby) meant a new user with no friends still saw live check-ins "and get value from day one". — [Braze / Appboy article](https://www.braze.com/resources/articles/engage-millions-users-mobile-community-building-lessons-untappd)

Polarsteps
- Origin: co-founder Niek Bokkers sailed the Atlantic solo and each day sent his coordinates by satellite phone to a server at home, which plotted one dot on a map; the dots of one trip formed the first prototype, and it "went a bit viral". Job Harmsen's Art & Technology graduation project became the first interface; the four founders (Harmsen, Bokkers, Droste, Neustadt) built it evenings and weekends. — [Job Harmsen, LinkedIn](https://nl.linkedin.com/in/jobharmsen)
- Droste: the idea came from Bokkers wanting family and friends to follow his position; once smartphones carried GPS, they built an app. First version live in the App Store in March 2015. — [Odido business blog](https://www.odido.nl/business-blog/?p=5125) (search snippet)
- March 2016 description of the product: tracks where you have been, shows trips on a world map, photos along the route, written notes; "some use it for personal memories, others to share their whereabouts with family and friends". — [Emerce, 18 March 2016](https://www.emerce.nl/?p=680408)

### Inferences
- Inference: in all three, the "shared content" was a by-product of the private log (a GPS file, a check-in, a location ping), so contributing cost the user nothing extra. None of them asked users to write for strangers at the start.
- Inference: Strava's leaderboard made a stranger's log valuable to you (someone to beat), which is why a handful of cyclists on two segments was enough to feel alive.

### Gaps
- No source describes what a Polarsteps user saw on day one in March 2015 beyond the map; the beta's social features at that point are undocumented in what I found.

## Key question 2: How did the first thousand users arrive?

### Takeaway
Strava hand-picked and equipped its first users (ten Garmin units to cyclists on two coasts in 2008, GPS units bought in bulk), then grew by word of mouth through group rides to about 20,000 by its January 2011 Series A. Untappd got its first users from Twitter and Foursquare circles and then a single Mashable article on 5 November 2010, two months after the private beta. Polarsteps' first "few hundred" users were friends-of-founders plus a Dutch startup-award audience in 2015, reaching 20,000 within a year with no advertising.

### Cited Findings
Strava
- 2008: founders sent ten Garmin head units to passionate cyclists on the East and West coasts, who became the first beta users; during the 2008 Tour de France they ran East Coast vs West Coast competitions among beta users to drive feedback and usage; first developers were hired after the beta reaction, some of them beta users. Bootstrapped with proceeds from the Kana exit. — [Techstadium](https://techstadium.substack.com/p/strava-analyzing-the-growth-playbook) (no original source named)
- The founders bought GPS devices in bulk from their local Costco and gave them to potential users; recruited through personal networks, cycling events and direct outreach. — [Alexander Jarvis, "Strava doing things that don't scale"](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/strava-doing-things-that-dont-scale) (secondary; sources unnamed)
- Another secondary account says Strava launched after "an initial test with 300 Garmin cycling computers". — [Mind Your Surroundings newsletter](https://mindyoursurroundings.beehiiv.com/p/strava); conflicts with the "ten head units" figure above; I could not find a primary source for either number.
- Gainey: "We're gonna go an inch wide with this one group, we're gonna go mile deep with them." — [Alexander Jarvis](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/strava-doing-things-that-dont-scale)
- Bootstrapped until 2011; raised a $3.5m institutional round after reaching about 20,000 users. — [Techstadium, citing VentureBeat 2011](https://techstadium.substack.com/p/strava-analyzing-the-growth-playbook); round announced 12 January 2011, led by Sigma Partners — [Wikipedia, citing PR Newswire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strava)
- Officially launched 2009 as a web platform for cyclists; founding listed January 2009. — [Contrary Research](https://research.contrary.com/company/strava)

Untappd
- Avola met Mather on Twitter; a Mashable employee, Christina Warren, checked in a beer ten days into the launch; the first article ran on Mashable on 5 November 2010 ("A Foursquare For Beer Lovers"). — [Greg Avola, Medium](https://medium.com/@gregavola/8-6992a7f093b7) (search snippet; page returned 403)
- Avola credits that Mashable article (around November 2010) as the tipping point; "not a PR campaign", it grew from one journalist's enthusiasm; "completely unexpected" and the team had to hurry to handle traffic. Growth came with no marketing budget, both founders still in full-time jobs on opposite coasts. — [Braze](https://www.braze.com/resources/articles/engage-millions-users-mobile-community-building-lessons-untappd)
- Numbers: at one year, 100,000 registered users, 150,000+ photos, 4 million+ check-ins; by February 2013, 400,000 users and 21 million+ check-ins; the original badge set had 50 icons. — [Leighton Hubbell, Coroflot portfolio](https://coroflot.com/leighton_hubbell/Icon-design) (search snippet; page returned 403; a designer's portfolio, treat the dates as approximate)

Polarsteps
- March 2015: first version in the App Store. — [Odido business blog](https://www.odido.nl/business-blog/?p=5125) (snippet)
- Accenture Innovation Awards 2015 (Seamless Travel & Transportation theme; winners announced 30 October 2015 in Utrecht; 800+ entries): Polarsteps reached the final of ten. They entered "mainly to pitch and gain visibility"; at the time the founders worked one day less a week for their employers and spent weekends on the app. Pitching to a room of about 200 people "likely contributed indirectly" to later investor interest. User base grew "from a few hundred since AIA 2015 to hundreds of thousands by December 2017". — [Emerce interview, 4 May 2018](https://www.emerce.nl/interviews/polarsteps-reis-plannen-tracken-n-bewaren); award context — [Silicon Canals, winners 2015](https://siliconcanals.com/accenture-innovation-awards-winnaars-van-2015/)
- 18 March 2016: "Without any advertising, the app gained tens of thousands of users in one year." Droste: "Afgelopen jaar hadden we twintigduizend gebruikers die samen 3,5 miljoen locaties bezochten." About 150,000 status updates posted. Growth "entirely organic, driven by word of mouth"; Droste pointed to App Store ratings "all four or five stars". — [Emerce, 18 March 2016](https://www.emerce.nl/?p=680408)
- 17 March 2016: the iPhone app was still a beta, live "less than a year"; "een zeer actieve community"; daily requests for an Android app; Polarsteps started at the incubator TMG Startups (now closed). — [Silicon Canals, 17 March 2016](https://siliconcanals.com/?p=28059)

### Inferences
- Inference: Strava's first thousand were effectively recruited one by one and subsidised with hardware; the leaderboard then did the inviting ("beat my time" needs the other person on the platform).
- Inference: Polarsteps' "few hundred" at the end of 2015 after seven months live suggests the award and friends network, not the App Store, delivered the first users; the jump to 20,000 by March 2016 coincides with travellers sharing links with family (the founding use case), but no source states the channel explicitly.
- Inference: Untappd is the only one of the three whose first thousand are attributable to one press moment within ten weeks of the private beta.

### Gaps
- No Strava source gives a user count between the 2008 beta and 20,000 in January 2011, nor names specific cycling clubs or forums.
- No Polarsteps source names a travel-blogger, forum or press channel for 2015; Dutch press on the app before March 2016 is absent from what I found (iCulture's May 2015 roundup does not mention it).
- Untappd's "100,000 at one year" is from a designer's portfolio, not the company.

## Key question 3: Which mechanics made people contribute and invite others?

### Takeaway
Strava: segment leaderboards and KOM-style comparison (then kudos after 2011), with the 2011 phone app removing the GPS-plus-laptop friction. Untappd: badges needing repeated check-ins, user votes on new badge waves, Nearby and Trending feeds, and breweries claiming their listing and putting up "check in here" signs. Polarsteps: the automatic map that family follows, then the printed Travel Book (launched 6 February 2017) which users had asked for and which became the whole revenue.

### Cited Findings
Strava
- Segments with UCI climb categories attached algorithmically, kudos as a social currency, and "matched runs" for runners were built in the social push after the 2011 round; the 2011 iOS app "sharply increased engagement" because rides could be uploaded from a phone instead of a GPS device and laptop; word of mouth from group rides credited. — [Techstadium](https://techstadium.substack.com/p/strava-analyzing-the-growth-playbook)
- Gainey: "Our focus was on engagement, not growth"; founders: "by focusing on engagement we actually saw that our growth actually accelerated". — [Alexander Jarvis](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/strava-doing-things-that-dont-scale)
- Strava Metro (2014) licensed aggregated member-logged data to 300+ city planning departments; London and Glasgow signed by May 2014. — [Techstadium](https://techstadium.substack.com/p/strava-analyzing-the-growth-playbook); [Wikipedia, citing Road.cc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strava)

Untappd
- Retention mechanics: users vote every two months on the next badge wave; badges need repeated check-ins (e.g. "Radler" badge needs five Radler check-ins); recommendations improved over time. Acquisition mechanics: breweries could claim their listing like Yelp and "many put up signs encouraging patrons to check in and rate beers". Nearby Beers, Trending Beers, Nearby Bars, Nearby Breweries, Top Rated Beers gave reasons to open the app. — [Braze](https://www.braze.com/resources/articles/engage-millions-users-mobile-community-building-lessons-untappd)
- First sponsored badge (Sam Adams) came about a year after launch. — [Untappd blog, "The Next Chapter"](https://untappd.com/blog/the-next-chapter/1197) (search snippet)
- Badges "have become promotional vehicles for craft brewers, pubs, companies and festivals". — [Coroflot portfolio](https://coroflot.com/leighton_hubbell/Icon-design) (snippet)

Polarsteps
- 18 March 2016: the printable photobook "built from photos, texts and locations" was "the second most requested feature" after Android. — [Emerce, 18 March 2016](https://www.emerce.nl/?p=680408)
- 6 February 2017: Travel Book launched, "an instant photo album generated after a trip ends", with route, statistics, weather and altitude; four colours, up to 300 pages, 24-page hardcover from €20 including worldwide shipping. — [Polarsteps press release, 6 Feb 2017](https://www.polarsteps.com/news/polarsteps-tracks-your-trip-and-turns-it-into-an-instant-photo-album)
- Bokkers: "The idea to offer photo books came from our users, not from us"; travel books were not on the original roadmap; "Monetization is a choice, and faced with choosing either growth or revenue from the app, we chose growth"; Peecho hardcover books account for 100% of app revenue (as of 23 Nov 2022); 9 million+ downloads since launch. — [Peecho case study, 23 Nov 2022](https://www.peecho.com/case-studies/polarsteps)
- 27 December 2017: "Gebruikers werven gebruikers en zeker internationaal groeit het steeds harder." — [Emerce, 27 Dec 2017](https://www.emerce.nl/interviews/koen-droste-polarsteps-voor-na-reis-alles-n-platform)

### Inferences
- Inference: none of the three used ambassadors or local champions in their first years; the closest is Untappd's brewery claiming (the venue as champion) and Strava's hand-recruited beta cyclists.
- Inference: Polarsteps' invite loop is structural: the traveller's family and friends are the audience the product was built for, so each trip recruits a following, and the printed book is a retention and revenue device rather than an acquisition one.

### Gaps
- Exact launch dates of Strava segments, KOM and kudos are not given by any source I found.
- No source quantifies the share of Untappd sign-ups attributable to brewery signage or badges.

## Key question 4: When did growth become organic, and what triggered it?

### Takeaway
Strava: the 2011 phone app (and the money from the January 2011 round) after two years of bootstrapped cyclist-only growth. Untappd: the 5 November 2010 Mashable article, after which Thursday-to-Saturday crashes were the problem and fixing them was, in Avola's words, the sign of product-market fit. Polarsteps: the March 2016 €500k round let the founders quit their jobs ("het keerpunt"); monthly actives went from 20,000 to 100,000 in the year to December 2017, with Apple's App of the Day in 18 countries and a WWDC mention, and zero marketing spend.

### Cited Findings
- Strava: iOS app 2011 "sharply increased engagement"; Series A 12 January 2011 after ~20,000 users; Horvath CEO 2010 to 2013. — [Techstadium](https://techstadium.substack.com/p/strava-analyzing-the-growth-playbook); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strava)
- Untappd: after the Mashable article, Thursday through Saturday nights became a recurring struggle with frequent crashes; "once those issues were resolved, the team felt they had product-market fit"; once the article's bump faded growth "depended on community building" and features that encouraged word of mouth. — [Braze](https://www.braze.com/resources/articles/engage-millions-users-mobile-community-building-lessons-untappd); the Thursday-to-Saturday overload "in its first couple years" is also in [CNBC, 25 April 2016](https://www.cnbc.com/2016/04/25/two-guys-created-their-dream-jobs-forged-by-a-love-for-tech-and-beer.html) (snippet; page 403)
- Untappd: founders left their day jobs only once the business was large enough (by April 2016). — [CNBC, 25 April 2016](https://www.cnbc.com/2016/04/25/two-guys-created-their-dream-jobs-forged-by-a-love-for-tech-and-beer.html) (snippet)
- Polarsteps: €500,000 from TMG and Silver Point Ventures (Jorrit Steinz), March 2016; Droste: "Na de investering van maart 2016. Dat was het keerpunt." Founders quit their jobs. August 2017: a further €900,000 from Dutch internet entrepreneurs. Apple named it App of the Day in 18 countries and mentioned it at WWDC. "The company spent nothing on marketing"; revenue went into product. Downsides: scaling the organisation took longer than expected, support mail rose sharply, servers needed rework, slowing features. — [Emerce, 4 May 2018](https://www.emerce.nl/interviews/polarsteps-reis-plannen-tracken-n-bewaren)
- Polarsteps, 27 December 2017: 250,000 accounts, 100,000 monthly actives versus 20,000 a year earlier; about 1.4 million raised; "Tot op heden hebben we nog geen cent aan marketing uitgegeven." — [Emerce, 27 Dec 2017](https://www.emerce.nl/interviews/koen-droste-polarsteps-voor-na-reis-alles-n-platform)
- Later scale, one line each: Strava ~50m registered end 2019, ~99m by May 2022 — [Fitt Insider](https://insider.fitt.co/137-michael-horvath-ceo-co-founder-of-strava/); Untappd ~2.8m registered users, 250m+ check-ins, 1,100 check-ins a minute on New Year's Eve (undated, Braze) — [Braze](https://www.braze.com/resources/articles/engage-millions-users-mobile-community-building-lessons-untappd); Polarsteps 22m+ travellers, 100+ team, profitable — [Job Harmsen, LinkedIn](https://nl.linkedin.com/in/jobharmsen).

### Inferences
- Inference: for all three the "organic" phase was really the phase after friction was removed (phone upload, server stability, founders full-time), not after a marketing trigger.

### Gaps
- The date of Polarsteps' Apple App of the Day featuring is not given (before May 2018).
- Lenny Rachitsky's "first 1,000 users" post does not cover any of the three apps; its taxonomy (offline where users are, online where users are, friends, FOMO, influencers, press, pre-launch community; most startups used one strategy, none more than three) is the only general frame. — [Lenny's Newsletter, May 2020](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-the-biggest-consumer-apps-got)

## Key question 5: What is documented about Polarsteps' early marketing from Amsterdam?

### Takeaway
The documented picture of 2015 to 2017 is: four founders with day jobs, an incubator (TMG Startups), one Dutch innovation-award final used purely for visibility, zero paid marketing, Dutch trade press (Emerce, Silicon Canals) only from the funding round in March 2016, and growth attributed by the founders to users recruiting users and App Store ratings; the Travel Book (2017) was user-requested and became the only revenue.

### Cited Findings
- Hobby project 2015, four founders building evenings and weekends; Harmsen did all design alone for five years after launch. — [Emerce, 27 Dec 2017](https://www.emerce.nl/interviews/koen-droste-polarsteps-voor-na-reis-alles-n-platform); [Job Harmsen, LinkedIn](https://nl.linkedin.com/in/jobharmsen)
- Started at incubator TMG Startups; TMG had already invested the year before March 2016; March 2016 money for a larger Amsterdam office, an Android developer, Android app that summer, experiments with trip-planning revenue and printed photo albums, and media partnerships in the Netherlands via TMG. — [Silicon Canals, 17 March 2016](https://siliconcanals.com/?p=28059); [Emerce, 18 March 2016](https://www.emerce.nl/?p=680408)
- Investor Jorrit Steinz cited "strong first usage figures" as the reason. — [Silicon Canals, 17 March 2016](https://siliconcanals.com/?p=28059)
- Dutch investors noticed them "later than the founders expected". — [Emerce, 4 May 2018](https://www.emerce.nl/interviews/polarsteps-reis-plannen-tracken-n-bewaren)
- Marketing spend: none through December 2017 and still "nothing" by May 2018. — [Emerce, 27 Dec 2017](https://www.emerce.nl/interviews/koen-droste-polarsteps-voor-na-reis-alles-n-platform); [Emerce, 4 May 2018](https://www.emerce.nl/interviews/polarsteps-reis-plannen-tracken-n-bewaren)

### Inferences
- Inference for a solo Dutch maker: Polarsteps' first year ran on the founders' own and their users' networks plus one pitch event, and the trade press only arrived with money. The thing that compounded was that every traveller's audience (family at home) was a non-user by definition.

### Gaps
- Nothing found in FD, NRC or Sprout on the 2015 to 2016 period; no documentation of travel-blogger outreach, Reddit, or App Store optimisation in those years.
- The "went a bit viral" claim for the dot-on-a-map prototype has no date, number or channel attached.
